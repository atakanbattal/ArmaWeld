What Is EN 1090-2 EXC2? Practical Differences Between EXC2 and EXC3

2026-11-16 9 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Most load-bearing steel structures in Europe are made in execution class EXC2: industrial buildings, warehouses, platforms, stairs, equipment support frames and similar. EXC2 is also the default class: under EN 1090-2, if no execution class is specified, EXC2 applies. This article explains what EXC2 requires in fabrication and what changes when you move to EXC3.

How Is the Execution Class Chosen?

The execution class is set by the structural designer, not the fabricator. Since EN 1090-2:2018, the selection rules sit in Annex C of EN 1993-1-1. Three parameters are used:

  • Consequence class (CC1, CC2, CC3): how serious loss of life, economic and environmental consequences would be if the structure failed (EN 1990).
  • Type of loading: mainly static, or fatigue and seismic effects?
  • Material and fabrication difficulty: high-strength steel, thick sections, special joints.

In practice, most statically loaded structures in CC2 are EXC2. CC3 structures, fatigue-loaded members and some seismic applications move up to EXC3. A structure does not have to be in a single class; specific members or joints can be given a different class.

The NDT rates are summarised from EN 1090-2 Table 24 and depend on the utilisation grade (U) of the weld. For transverse butt welds in tension with U ≥ 0.5, EXC2 requires 10 % and EXC3 20 % supplementary testing. For welds with low utilisation, EXC2 may need no supplementary NDT. These rates come on top of visual inspection, which is 100 % in every class.

Welding Coordination: Who Is Responsible?

EN 1090-2 links the technical knowledge of the welding coordinator (EN ISO 14731) to steel group, thickness and execution class. In EXC2, basic knowledge (usually welding technician level) can be enough for thin and medium S235–S355 sections; specific knowledge is required as thickness and strength increase. In EXC3, at least specific knowledge is needed, and comprehensive knowledge (welding engineer level) for high-strength steels.

For the buyer, this means the coordinator scope stated on your supplier's certificate must cover the steel grade and thickness of your part.

Welding Procedures and Welders

In both classes, welds are made to a qualified welding procedure (WPS), welders are qualified to EN ISO 9606-1 and welding operators to EN ISO 14732. The difference lies in how the procedure is qualified. In EXC2, a standard welding procedure (ISO 15612) or previous experience (ISO 15611) may be used in some cases. In EXC3, the procedure must be qualified by a procedure test (ISO 15614-1) or a pre-production test (ISO 15613).

EXC3 may also call for production tests and additional hardness measurements for certain steels and thicknesses.

Traceability: Partial vs Full

In EXC2, material certificates can be linked to a batch or delivery. You know which heats were used in an order, but you do not have to record which plate each part was cut from. In EXC3, every main part must be traceable to its material certificate through all stages of fabrication. That means extra work in nesting, part marking and records, and it shows in the price.

Material Documents

Regardless of execution class, EN 1090-2 requires at least a 2.2 document for S235 and a 3.1 inspection certificate for S275 and higher (EN 10204). In practice, most steel suppliers issue 3.1 certificates for everything. Stating this clearly in the order is the easiest way to avoid problems.

When Is EXC2 Enough, and When Not?

  • Enough: statically loaded building frames, platforms, stairs and railings, equipment support frames, industrial structures.
  • Take care: members exposed to vibration or variable loads (crane girders, conveyor supports) may require EXC3 based on the fatigue calculation.
  • Not enough: large structures with crowds of people, bridges and high-consequence structures are usually EXC3.

Choosing a higher class than needed raises cost; choosing a lower class shifts responsibility to the designer and buyer. The class should match the design calculation and be written clearly in the specification.

What to State in the Order and on the Drawing

  • "EN 1090-2, EXC2" and a list of any members in a different class
  • Steel grade and quality (for example S355J2+N) and the type of inspection document required
  • Tolerance class (EN 1090-2 functional tolerance class 1 or 2)
  • Surface preparation and corrosion protection requirements
  • Whether CE marking and a Declaration of Performance (DoP) are required

Frequently Asked Questions

Which class applies if the specification states none?

Under EN 1090-2, EXC2 applies when no execution class is specified. It is still recommended that the designer states the class explicitly.

Can an EXC3-certified company produce EXC2 work?

Yes. A fabricator certified for a higher class can also produce lower-class work. The reverse does not apply: an EXC2-certified company cannot produce EXC3 work.

How large is the price difference between EXC2 and EXC3?

It depends on the part. Most of the difference comes from extra welding and repair time for quality level B, a higher NDT rate and full traceability records. It is small on simple parts and noticeable on heavily welded ones.

Is CE marking mandatory for EXC2 parts?

For structural steel components within the scope of EN 1090-1 placed on the EU market, CE marking and a Declaration of Performance are mandatory. Machine parts outside that scope do not need it.
