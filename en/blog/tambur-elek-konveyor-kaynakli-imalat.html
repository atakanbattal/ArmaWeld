Trommel Screen and Conveyor Fabrication: Welded Structures That Rotate and Carry

2026-11-16 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Recycling plants, compost and waste sorting lines, mining and aggregate sites, agriculture and food processing: in all of them material is moved and separated by size. Trommel screens and conveyors are the backbone of this work. Two challenges stand out for welded fabrication: the precision of rotating structures and fatigue under constant vibration.

What Is a Trommel Screen?

A cylindrical screen rotating about a slightly inclined horizontal axis. Material enters at one end; as it turns, fines fall through the screen surface and oversize leaves at the other end. A trommel typically consists of:

  • A cylindrical shell or supporting cage,
  • Perforated plate or replaceable screen panels,
  • Riding rings (tyres) or a shaft and bearing assembly,
  • Internal lifters and spirals,
  • Feed and discharge hoppers, support frame and guards.

Precision in the Drum Shell

  • Roundness and straightness: rolling quality and segment alignment determine runout during rotation.
  • Ring concentricity: riding rings must be concentric and square to the drum axis so the rollers contact properly. Ring faces and seating diameters are therefore often machined after welding (see machining after welding).
  • Distortion control: on long drums, symmetrical weld sequences and positioners reduce axial bowing.
  • Balance: uneven mass distribution causes vibration; symmetrical placement of lifters and stiffeners matters.

Screen Surface

The screen surface is either rolled perforated plate or replaceable panels bolted to a cage. Replaceable panels make maintenance and changes of aperture easier. When welding perforated plate, watch for crack initiation near hole edges; place welds in solid bands where possible.

Fatigue

A trommel goes through a load cycle with every revolution, which adds up to a very high number of cycles over its life. Critical points:

  • Connection plates and welds between rings and shell,
  • Weld ends of internal lifters on the shell,
  • Nodes in cage structures.

Smoothing weld toes, preferring continuous over intermittent welds and filling craters extend life noticeably (see fatigue and FAT classes).

Wear

Aggregate, glass, metal and construction waste are abrasive. Wear-resistant steel plates or replaceable liners can be used in feed hoppers, lifters and conveying surfaces. Preheat and heat input limits must be respected when welding these steels (see welding wear plate).

Modular Design and Transport

Long conveyors and large drums are split into modules sized for transport. Module joints use flanges and bolts; post-weld flatness of flanges and hole positions are critical for trouble-free site assembly. A trial assembly in the shop reduces surprises on site.

Surface Protection

Hot-dip galvanising is common for outdoor plants; vent holes must be planned in the design. For indoor use or parts too large for the galvanising bath, a paint system is used (see galvanising welded parts).

Documentation

  • Dimensional report: drum runout, ring concentricity, frame diagonals,
  • Material certificates,
  • Welding and inspection records,
  • Coating thickness reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most critical dimension on a trommel?

Concentricity and squareness of the riding rings to the drum axis; proper roller contact depends on it.

Why is a trommel fatigue-sensitive?

Every revolution is a load cycle, adding up to a very high number of cycles over its life.

Should the screen surface be welded or bolted?

Replaceable bolted panels ease maintenance and aperture changes; welded perforated plate is simpler and cheaper.

How are long conveyors transported?

They are split into modules to transport size and joined on site with flanged, bolted connections.
