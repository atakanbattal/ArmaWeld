Sourcing Welded Sheet Metal Assemblies: Buy Piece by Piece or Complete?

2026-01-26 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A machine cabin, a panel frame or a conveyor side plate is rarely a single part: laser-cut sheets, press-brake bent edges, welded brackets and finally paint. From a purchasing point of view the question is: will you buy these steps from separate suppliers, or receive the assembly complete under one party's responsibility?

Two Models

Step-by-step sourcingComplete assembly
Number of contactsCutter, bender, welder, painterOne responsible supplier
Intermediate logisticsParts travel between companiesCoordinated by the supplier

Purchasing workloadHighLow

Why Tolerance Stack-Up Matters

±0.2 mm in cutting, ±0.5 mm plus angle deviation in bending, shrinkage in welding... Even if each step is within its own tolerance, the deviations add up and the finished assembly may not fit. With step-by-step sourcing, the "whose fault is it" discussion begins. With complete sourcing, the welder adjusts the fixture to the real dimensions of the bent parts, feeds back to cutting if needed and is responsible for the finished assembly (see sheet bending tolerances, ISO 13920).

Why the Welding Shop Should Be at the Centre

In a sheet metal assembly the step that needs the most know-how and responsibility is usually welding: distortion control, fixturing, weld sequence, penetration and appearance. Cutting and bending are standard services and can be managed reliably with good partners. That is why a model in which the welding company coordinates cutting and bending naturally puts the final dimensional responsibility in one place.

Design Points

  • Enough flange length and torch access on edges to be welded after bending.
  • Tab-and-slot details that ease assembly; they reduce tacking and fix dimensions.
  • Intermittent welds and correct weld lengths to limit heat input in thin sheet.
  • Drain and vent holes if the part will be painted or galvanised.
  • Dimensioning critical features from the assembly interfaces, not from the welded body.

What to Include in an RFQ

  • The assembly drawing and all sub-part drawings, 3D model (STEP) and flat patterns (DXF) if available.
  • Materials and thicknesses, surface treatment (powder coating, galvanising, e-coat etc.).
  • Annual volume and quantity per order.
  • Critical dimensions and how they are checked.
  • Packaging and delivery terms (see export packaging and Incoterms).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is buying a sheet metal assembly from one supplier more expensive?

The total price can be similar; total cost usually falls because intermediate logistics, purchasing workload and fit-up risk are reduced.

Can a price breakdown be requested for a complete assembly?

Yes. Cutting, bending, welding and surface treatment costs can be shown separately.

Who is responsible for tolerance problems?

With complete sourcing, one supplier is responsible for the dimensions of the finished assembly.

Which files are needed for a quote?

Assembly and sub-part drawings, STEP and DXF if available, material, surface treatment, volume and critical dimensions.
