A.TR or EUR.1? Movement and Origin Documents for Exported Welded Parts

2026-07-27 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

When welded parts ship from Türkiye to Europe, the right movement or origin document next to the invoice decides whether the buyer pays customs duty. The wrong document can hold a shipment at customs or leave the buyer with a duty bill later. This article gives a general framework; confirm each shipment with your customs broker.

What Is A.TR?

The A.TR movement certificate shows that industrial goods covered by the EU–Türkiye Customs Union are in free circulation. An important detail: A.TR is not a certificate of origin. It shows that the goods are in free circulation in Türkiye (either of Turkish origin or with duties paid), not where they were made. Industrial goods shipped with A.TR enter the EU free of customs duty.

What Is EUR.1?

EUR.1 is a preferential origin certificate. Under free trade agreements it shows that the goods meet the agreement’s rules of origin and can benefit from reduced or zero duty. For consignments below a value threshold or for approved exporters, an origin declaration on the invoice can replace EUR.1.

The Key Steel Exception: ECSC Products

The Customs Union does not cover everything. Agricultural products and products of the former European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC) are excluded. ECSC products are mainly iron and steel products of HS chapter 72: semi-finished goods such as plate, sheet, sections and bars. They are covered by a separate EU–Türkiye free trade agreement and travel not with A.TR but, if they meet the rules of origin, with EUR.1 or an origin declaration.

Welded parts, however, are usually classified as worked products in other headings: for example 7308 (steel structures and parts), 7326 (other articles of iron or steel) or as machine parts in chapter 84. These are mostly within the Customs Union and travel with A.TR. But some headings in chapter 73 also remain ECSC products. So the first step in choosing a document is classifying the product under the correct HS code.

Why HS Classification Matters

  • It determines which document (A.TR or EUR.1) is needed.
  • It determines whether trade measures such as EU steel safeguards or anti-dumping duties apply.
  • It determines whether CBAM applies (see the CBAM guide).

The same part may fall under a different heading depending on whether it is described as a machine part or a structural element. Agreeing the code between buyer and exporter avoids later problems.

Norway and Other EFTA Countries

Norway is not in the EU and not in the Customs Union, so A.TR is not valid there. Under the EFTA–Türkiye free trade agreement, preferential duty requires the goods to meet the rules of origin and travel with EUR.1 or an origin declaration on the invoice. Rules of origin depend on the share of non-originating inputs (for example sheet bought from outside the region) or whether sufficient working took place.

Preferential vs Non-Preferential Origin

Preferential origin is for duty reduction. Non-preferential origin defines where a product “comes from” and is used for anti-dumping measures, quotas or the buyer’s own supply chain reporting. Some buyers ask, beyond the product, where the steel was melted and rolled; material certificates and traceability records matter here.

Common Mistakes

  • Shipping to Norway with an A.TR.
  • Issuing an A.TR for an ECSC product.
  • Product description on the invoice not matching the HS code.
  • Issuing EUR.1 or an origin declaration without checking the rules of origin; in a later verification, the duty difference falls on the buyer.
  • Sending the document after the shipment rather than with it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is A.TR a certificate of origin?

No. A.TR is a movement certificate showing industrial goods are in free circulation in Türkiye; under the Customs Union it gives duty exemption in the EU.

Does steel sheet travel with A.TR?

No. Chapter 72 semi-finished steel products are ECSC products outside the Customs Union; if they meet the rules of origin they travel with EUR.1 or an origin declaration.

Which document for Norway?

Norway is not in the Customs Union. Under the EFTA–Türkiye agreement, EUR.1 or an origin declaration on the invoice is used.

How is the document chosen?

By the product’s HS classification and destination country. Confirm each shipment with a customs broker.
