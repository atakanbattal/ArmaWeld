Weld Joint Preparation: V, X, U and Bevel Grooves to ISO 9692-1

2026-02-11 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The quality of a butt weld is decided before the arc is struck. Groove geometry directly affects penetration, weld metal volume, number of runs, distortion and the risk of defects. Recommended joint preparations for steels are defined in EN ISO 9692-1. ISO 9692-2 covers submerged arc welding and ISO 9692-3 aluminium.

Dimensions That Define a Groove

  • Groove angle (α): total included angle between the two bevel faces.
  • Bevel angle (β): bevel angle on one part; used for single-bevel and U preparations.
  • Root gap (b): gap between the parts, providing root penetration.
  • Root face (c): unbevelled land that prevents burn-through.
  • Root radius (r): radius at the bottom of U and J grooves.

Why Weld Metal Volume Matters

On 20 mm plate, choosing a symmetric X instead of a 60° V roughly halves the cross-section to fill. That means less wire, fewer runs and less heat input, and symmetric runs balance angular distortion. On thicker plate a U groove cuts the cross-section significantly compared with a V, but needs machining, so preparation costs more. The choice depends on thickness, quantity and whether the part can be turned.

The Root Side: Backing, Back Gouging and One-Sided Welding

  • Ceramic or steel backing: forms a sound root when the back is not accessible. Permanent steel backing can act as a notch for fatigue and should be approved on the drawing.
  • Back gouging: in two-sided welding, after the first side the root is opened to sound metal by carbon arc or grinding and welded from the other side.
  • One-sided full penetration: root gap and root face must be held to tight tolerances, otherwise burn-through or lack of penetration follows.

Grooves for Submerged Arc Welding

The deep penetration of SAW allows a larger root face and narrower groove angle. For some thicknesses even unbevelled two-sided welding is possible. A groove prepared for manual MAG can therefore mean unnecessary weld metal under SAW. ISO 9692-2 gives separate recommendations for this process.

Common Preparation Mistakes

  1. Varying root gap: if the gap is not checked before tacking, penetration varies along the seam.
  2. Linear misalignment: the two surfaces are not flush; ISO 5817 limits this by quality level.
  3. Oxide and dross on cut faces: if the oxide layer from thermal cutting is not ground off, porosity and lack of fusion become more likely.
  4. No groove details on the drawing: writing only "full penetration" leaves open who chooses the groove and how.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should the V groove angle be?

In ISO 9692-1 the included angle of a V groove is usually 40-60°. The exact value depends on process, thickness and the approved WPS.

When is an X groove preferred?

On plate above about 10 mm when the part can be turned. An X groove needs about half the weld metal of a V and balances distortion.

What is the root face for?

The unbevelled land prevents burn-through at the root and gives a more stable fill, especially in SAW or two-sided welding.

Which processes does ISO 9692-1 cover?

Manual metal arc, gas-shielded, TIG and similar processes on steels. ISO 9692-2 covers SAW and ISO 9692-3 aluminium.
