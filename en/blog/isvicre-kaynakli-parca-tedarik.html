Sourcing Welded Parts for Switzerland: A Demanding Market Outside the EU

2026-02-20 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

With its precision machinery, food and packaging machine builders, energy, crane and special equipment manufacturers, Switzerland has some of the highest quality expectations in Europe. Labour costs are also very high, so part of its weld-intensive housings, frames and structures is sourced abroad. Switzerland is neither an EU nor an EEA member, which creates differences in the supply chain that need attention.

Customs: EUR.1, Not A.TR

The Turkey-EU customs union does not cover Switzerland. Under the free trade agreement between Turkey and the EFTA states, preferential origin is usually proven with an EUR.1 movement certificate or an origin declaration on the invoice (see A.TR, EUR.1 and origin). Whether the product meets the rules of origin should be checked before shipment. Switzerland charges import VAT; the customs declaration is usually made by the buyer's customs broker.

Road shipments transit through EU territory. That means transit documents and a separate customs procedure at the Swiss border, and the time must be added to the delivery plan (see packaging and Incoterms).

Technical Rules and Expectations

Swiss product rules are aligned with the EU in many areas; in machinery, CE-marked products are generally accepted. Still, confirm with the buyer which rules apply to each project. In practice the technical expectations resemble those of Germany and Austria:

  • Welding quality management to EN ISO 3834 and EN ISO 9606-1 welder certificates (see EN ISO 3834-2).
  • EN 1090 and the appropriate execution class for load-bearing steel structures.
  • EN 10204 3.1 material certificates and full traceability.
  • Dimensional reports that record actual values for critical dimensions (see dimensional tolerances).
  • Clean welds, neat grinding and good paint quality on visible surfaces.

Language and Business Culture

Switzerland has German-, French- and Italian-speaking regions; most industry is in the German-speaking part. Technical correspondence runs in German or English. Swiss buyers place great value on punctuality, precision and predictability: a delivery date that is kept, correct documentation and timely notice of problems are the basis of trust. Price matters, but reliability is usually more decisive.

Setting Up the First Cooperation

  1. Share quality documents and sample reports.
  2. Clarify origin and customs with the logistics provider.
  3. Start with a first article approval and agree the report format with the buyer (see first article approval).
  4. Give a realistic delivery date and share production status regularly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the A.TR used for welded parts to Switzerland?

No. Switzerland is not in the EU customs union; under the Turkey-EFTA agreement preferential origin is usually shown with an EUR.1 or invoice declaration.

Does Switzerland accept CE marking?

Swiss product rules are aligned with the EU in many areas and CE-marked machinery is generally accepted; confirm per project with the buyer.

Are there extra steps for road shipments to Switzerland?

Yes. Goods transit through the EU and a separate customs procedure takes place at the Swiss border.

What do Swiss buyers value most?

Punctuality, precision, complete documentation and predictable communication.
