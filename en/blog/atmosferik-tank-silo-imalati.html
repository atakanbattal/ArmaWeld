Atmospheric Tank and Silo Fabrication: Welding, Tightness and Standards

2026-10-13 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Water, fuel, chemicals, food liquids, grain, cement or plastic granules: all are stored in tanks or silos. These structures usually operate at atmospheric pressure, but being “unpressurised” does not make weld quality unimportant. A leaking tank is an environmental and safety problem; a cracked silo hopper means a production stoppage.

Tank or Pressure Vessel?

In Europe, vessels with a maximum allowable pressure above 0.5 bar fall under the Pressure Equipment Directive (PED). Atmospheric or very low-pressure tanks are outside its scope and are designed and built to their own standards. Determining scope is the designer’s job; see our separate article on pressure vessel fabrication.

Which standard applies depends on use, country and customer specification, and should be stated on the drawing.

Shop or Site?

Tanks and silos small enough to travel by road can be fully built and tested in the shop. Larger structures are erected on site; the shop prepares cut, rolled and, where needed, welded shell segments, bottom plates, roof members, nozzles and reinforcements. For quality, shop welding has the advantage of a controlled environment.

Shell and Bottom Welds

  • Shell seams: longitudinal and circumferential butt welds are usually full penetration; radiographic or ultrasonic coverage is set by the standard and design.
  • Bottom plates: on large tanks they may be lap-welded; tightness is the key issue.
  • Shell-to-bottom joint: inner and outer fillet welds; a highly stressed area that needs careful inspection.
  • Roundness: the rolling quality of shell segments directly affects misalignment and weld quality at erection.

Nozzles and Reinforcing Pads

Every opening weakens the shell. Above a certain diameter, nozzles get a reinforcing pad. A “tell-tale hole” in the pad allows the welds between pad and shell to be leak-tested with low-pressure air and soap solution. Nozzle flange dimensions and orientation are critical for piping connections.

Tightness Tests

TestApplied to
Hydrostatic fill testWhole tank; shell seams and strength
Vacuum box testBottom welds and accessible seams
Low-pressure air and soap testReinforcing pads, roof seams
Penetrant or chalk-and-kerosene testAreas such as the shell-to-bottom fillet weld

Test types and acceptance criteria follow the applicable standard and should be collected in an inspection and test plan (ITP).

Silo-Specific Issues

  • Hopper: the hopper angle governs material flow; the hopper-to-shell junction carries high loads and its weld quality is critical.
  • Wear: with abrasive materials, wear-resistant liner plates can be added in the hopper and outlet (see welding wear plate).
  • Loads: discharge creates dynamic pressures; supports and connections are designed for them.
  • Inner surface: internal seams may need grinding so material does not stick.

Material and Surface Protection

  • For water, oil and dry bulk: usually S235 or S355 structural steel,
  • For food, drinking water and chemicals: stainless steel (see stainless fabrication),
  • Outside: a paint system for the site’s corrosivity category (see ISO 12944),
  • Inside: a lining suited to the stored product; drinking water and food may need a compliance certificate.

Documentation

  • Material certificates and plate traceability,
  • WPS, WPQR and welder certificates,
  • NDT and tightness test reports,
  • Dimensional reports (roundness, verticality, nozzle positions),
  • Coating thickness reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an atmospheric tank covered by the PED?

Vessels with a maximum allowable pressure not above 0.5 bar are outside PED scope; the designer determines scope.

How is tank weld tightness tested?

With a hydrostatic fill test, vacuum box on bottom welds, and low-pressure air and soap on reinforcing pads, among other methods.

Which tanks can be built in the shop?

Tanks and silos small enough for road transport; larger ones are erected on site.

What matters in a silo hopper?

Hopper angle, weld quality at the hopper-to-shell junction, wear and dynamic loads during discharge.
