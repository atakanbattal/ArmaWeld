Dust Collector Housing Fabrication: Tube Sheet, Tightness and Pressure Difference

2026-05-18 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Companies building dust collectors (pulse-jet baghouse, cartridge) usually focus on design, filter media, pneumatics and controls, and have the large welded housing made outside. The housing looks like a simple sheet metal box but directly affects performance: a distorted tube sheet causes bag leakage, and a leak in the housing causes suction loss.

Housing Sections

  • Dirty air plenum: where bags or cartridges hang; the inlet baffle sits here.
  • Tube sheet: the plate with bag or cartridge holes separating dirty and clean sides.
  • Clean air plenum: the top plenum with blowpipes and service doors.
  • Hopper: conical or pyramidal hopper with discharge flange.
  • Support legs and platforms: structure carrying the housing and service access.

The Tube Sheet: the Most Critical Part

Hole diameter, pitch and plate flatness determine how bag/cartridge seals seat. If the plate distorts during welding, seals do not seat fully and dust bypasses to the clean side. So the tube sheet is usually laser cut, stiffened at its edges and welded in with a symmetrical sequence (see weld distortion). Flatness should be measured and reported after welding.

Pressure Difference and Stiffening

On the suction side of a fan, the housing runs under negative pressure and large flat panels can collapse inwards. Panel thickness and external stiffener spacing are chosen by design pressure. For explosible dusts, explosion vent frames and housing strength are assessed separately; this is the designer's responsibility.

Tightness and Surface

  • All external seams continuous and tight; intermittent welds only on internal stiffeners.
  • Door and flange faces flat for gasket seating.
  • External paint chosen by corrosivity category (see ISO 12944); internal protection needed with humid or corrosive dust.

Modular Shipping

Large collectors are split into modules for transport: hopper, housing, plenum. If inter-module flanges are trial-fitted and marked in the shop, site erection goes smoothly. For duct connections, see industrial ductwork.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most critical part of a filter housing?

The tube sheet; hole accuracy and flatness determine seal seating.

Why does a housing collapse?

Negative pressure on the fan suction side; flat panels collapse if thickness or stiffening is insufficient.

How is tube sheet distortion prevented?

Laser cutting, edge stiffening and symmetrical welding; flatness measured after welding.

How are large collectors shipped?

As hopper, housing and plenum modules; flanges trial-fitted in the shop.
