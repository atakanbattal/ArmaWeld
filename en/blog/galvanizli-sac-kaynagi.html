Welding Galvanized Steel: Porosity, Zinc Fumes and Coating Repair

2026-11-16 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Welding galvanized material comes up in two situations: welding pre-coated sheet (continuous hot-dip or electro-galvanized) and welding parts that have already been hot-dip galvanized. In both, the root of the problem is the same: zinc vaporizes long before steel melts.

Why Problems Occur

Zinc melts at about 420 °C and boils at around 907 °C. When the arc takes the area above 1500 °C, zinc vapour tries to escape through the weld pool. The results:

  • Porosity: vapour is trapped in the solidifying pool, especially in fillet and lap joints between two coated surfaces.
  • Spatter and arc instability: the vapour disturbs the arc and the weld becomes irregular.
  • Coating loss around the weld: zinc burns off in the heat-affected zone and corrosion protection is lost.
  • Health risk: inhaled zinc oxide fume can cause a feverish condition known as metal fume fever.

Remove the Coating or Weld Through It?

For structural and load-bearing welds, the most reliable route is to grind the coating off locally in the weld area. Porosity and spatter fall sharply and weld quality approaches that of uncoated steel. The width of the cleaned band is planned at a few centimetres depending on the weld. Where removal is not possible, the measures below apply for welding through the coating.

Measures When Welding Through the Coating

  • An escape route for vapour: a small gap (about 1–1.5 mm) between parts in lap and T joints lets vapour escape.
  • Travel speed: moving slightly slower gives vapour time to leave before the pool solidifies.
  • Torch angle and technique: a slight push angle and short arc help zinc vaporize ahead of the pool.
  • Gas and wire: wires with higher silicon and manganese and a suitable Ar-CO₂ mix reduce porosity (see MIG/MAG parameters). Pulsed arc lowers spatter.
  • Procedure qualification: for load-bearing welds on coated material, the WPS may need to be verified on coated test pieces.

MIG Brazing on Thin Sheet

A common solution for automotive, ventilation and thin coated sheet is MIG brazing. The parent metal is not melted; a copper-based wire with a low melting point (e.g. CuSi3 or CuAl8) makes the joint. Because heat input is low, the zinc coating is far less damaged and corrosion protection around the joint is largely preserved. Strength is lower than a steel weld, however; suitability for load-bearing joints must be assessed in design.

Coating Repair After Welding

Zinc lost at the weld must be restored. For hot-dip galvanizing, ISO 1461 describes repair with zinc-rich paint or thermal zinc spraying and limits the repairable area: the total repaired area should not exceed 0.5% of the surface, and a single area 10 cm². The repair coating is usually required to be 30 µm thicker than the local coating requirement. Larger areas may require regalvanizing the part.

Wherever possible, the better route is to weld first and galvanize afterwards (see designing welded parts for galvanizing).

Health and Safety

Welding galvanized material requires local fume extraction at the weld and good ventilation. Suitable respiratory protection should be used in confined areas. Grinding off the coating greatly reduces fume before welding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does welding galvanized steel cause porosity?

Zinc vaporizes at about 907 °C and the vapour becomes trapped in the weld pool, especially in lap and fillet joints between two coated surfaces.

Should galvanized steel be ground before welding?

For load-bearing welds, removing the coating locally in the weld area is the most reliable method and sharply reduces porosity and spatter.

What is MIG brazing?

Joining with a copper-based wire such as CuSi3 without melting the parent metal. Low heat input spares the zinc coating, but strength is lower than a steel weld.

How is galvanizing repaired after welding?

To ISO 1461 with zinc-rich paint or thermal zinc spray; total repaired area should not exceed 0.5% of the surface and a single area 10 cm².
