Incoming Inspection in Welded Fabrication: Checking Material, Consumables and Bought-In Parts

2026-08-19 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The quality of a welded part starts with the material that enters the workshop. A plate of the wrong grade, wire of the wrong classification or a cut part with drifted dimensions spoils the result however well the welding is done. Incoming inspection is the cheapest way to catch these errors before production.

Scope of Incoming Inspection

  • Base material: plate, sheet, sections, tube,
  • Consumables: wire, electrodes, flux, shielding gas,
  • Cut, bent and machined parts from partners,
  • Purchased standard parts: bolts, bushes, flanges etc.

1. Document Check

The first step is checking the documents against the order:

  • Is the certificate type as ordered (2.2, 3.1, 3.2)? See EN 10204 certificates.
  • Are the steel grade and delivery condition right (e.g. S355J2+N)?
  • Are chemical composition and mechanical values within the standard?
  • Are required extras (Z25/Z35, impact test at a given temperature, ultrasonic testing) on the certificate?
  • Does the heat number on the certificate match the marking on the material?

2. Dimensions and Thickness

For hot-rolled plate, thickness tolerance follows EN 10029; if the tolerance class (A, B, C, D) is not specified in the order, class A usually applies. On thin and high-strength sheet, thickness deviation directly affects bending and joint geometry. Check:

  • Thickness (at several points),
  • Width, length and squareness,
  • Flatness; for sections, straightness and twist.

3. Surface Condition

Surface discontinuities of plate and sections (scratches, laps, scale) are assessed to EN 10163. Rust grade (A, B, C, D in ISO 8501-1), oil and paint residues are also checked. Heavily rusted material that has been stored outdoors affects both weld quality and preparation for painting.

4. Consumables

  • Is the wire, electrode or flux classification as specified in the WPS?
  • Is there a lot number and certificate?
  • Is the packaging intact; any damp or crushed spools?
  • For basic electrodes and fluxes, are storage and drying conditions in place (see hydrogen control)?
  • Is the shielding gas mixture and its certificate correct?

5. Parts from Partners

Laser, plasma or oxy-fuel cut parts and bent or machined parts also go through incoming inspection:

  • Critical dimensions and hole positions,
  • Cut surface quality, burr and dross,
  • Bevel angle and root face,
  • Bend angle and inside radius,
  • Heat number marking transferred to the part after cutting.

First orders or new parts get a fuller check; repeat work can be sampled.

6. Nonconforming Material

Nonconforming material or parts are marked, moved to a separate area (quarantine) and kept out of production. A nonconformity report is raised with the supplier and the decision (return, rework, use with customer concession) is recorded (see NCR and 8D).

7. Carrying Traceability Forward

EN 1090-2 requires constituent products in EXC3 and EXC4 to be traceable at every stage to their inspection documents; traceability is expected in EXC2 too. So the record number assigned at incoming inspection should travel with the part through cutting, welding and shipping (see digital traceability).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is checked first?

The documents: certificate type, steel grade, values and whether the heat number on the certificate matches the material marking.

Which standard sets plate thickness tolerance?

EN 10029 for hot-rolled plate. If no tolerance class is specified, class A usually applies.

What happens to nonconforming material?

It is marked, quarantined, a nonconformity is raised with the supplier and the decision is recorded.

What is checked on consumables?

Classification against the WPS, lot and certificate, packaging condition and storage conditions for moisture-sensitive consumables.
