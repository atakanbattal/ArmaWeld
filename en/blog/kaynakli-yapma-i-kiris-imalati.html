Welded Plate Girder Fabrication: Built-Up I-Beams When Rolled Sections Fall Short

2026-07-06 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Rolled section catalogues stop at certain depths and thicknesses. When you need a deeper beam, thicker flanges, a tapered section or different grades in flange and web, the answer is to build the beam from plate by welding. Crane runway beams, portal frames, bridge girders and heavy machine bases are typical uses.

Why a Plate Girder?

  • Section freedom: web depth, flange width and thickness follow the calculation; no excess weight.
  • Tapered sections: depth following the moment diagram saves material.
  • Supply: where heavy rolled sections are unavailable or have long lead times, plate is easier to source.

Web-to-Flange Welds

The longitudinal web-to-flange welds are usually double fillet welds; high loads or fatigue may call for partial or full penetration. Submerged arc welding (SAW) is efficient for long, straight seams (see submerged arc welding). Weld type and size come from the calculation; the fabricator reads them from the drawing and applies them with a qualified WPS.

Distortion and Camber

Concentrated heat from one side tilts the flanges and bows the girder. A balanced welding sequence, pre-setting and controlled straightening where needed keep dimensions (see welding distortion). For long girders the camber shown on the drawing is cut into the web plate; adding it afterwards with heat is harder.

Splices, Stiffeners and Thick Plate

  • Flange and web splices are staggered and made with full penetration butt welds, usually subject to ultrasonic testing.
  • Web stiffeners go at supports and point loads; corner snipes (weld access cut-outs) should be defined on the drawing.
  • Thick flanges loaded through thickness may need Z-quality steel (see lamellar tearing).

Tolerances and Documents

European structural projects apply EN 1090-2 manufacturing tolerances for section dimensions, flange tilt, web flatness and straightness (see EN 1090 EXC2). Delivery normally includes a dimensional report, material certificates, WPS/WPQR and NDT reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is a plate girder used?

When rolled sections are insufficient, a tapered section is wanted, or heavy sections are hard to source.

How are web-to-flange welds made?

Usually double fillet welds; SAW is efficient for long seams.

How is camber produced?

Usually cut into the web plate profile.

How are splices inspected?

Full penetration butt welds are usually ultrasonically tested.
