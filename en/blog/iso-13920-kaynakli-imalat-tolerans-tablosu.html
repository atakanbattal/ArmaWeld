ISO 13920 General Tolerances: Tolerance Table for Welded Constructions and Choosing the Right Class

2025-12-05 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Welding heats a part and it shrinks as it cools. That is why the dimensions of a weldment cannot be held as tightly as those of a machined part. Instead of writing a tolerance on every dimension, welded constructions usually get a general tolerance to ISO 13920. This article explains how to read the table, what a note like "ISO 13920-BF" means and when to choose which class.

What Does ISO 13920 Cover?

EN ISO 13920 defines general tolerances for welded structures, joints and components. There are two groups:

  • Length and angle tolerances: classes A, B, C and D. A is the tightest, D the widest.
  • Straightness, flatness and parallelism: classes E, F, G and H. E is the tightest, H the widest.

Both letters are written together on the drawing. ISO 13920-BF, for example, means class B for lengths and angles and class F for form tolerances. BF is the most common combination in practice.

The tolerance is selected by the longest dimension of the surface or edge concerned. A 2 m mounting surface may be out of flat by 4.5 mm in class F.

Angular Tolerances

Angular tolerances depend on the length of the shorter leg. In class B, they are ±45' for a short leg up to 400 mm, ±30' from 400 to 1,000 mm and ±20' above 1,000 mm. Class A is tighter, C and D wider. The angular deviation can also be expressed in mm, which makes measuring long parts easier.

The values are summarised from the standard. For contractual values, use the text of the edition referenced on the drawing.

Which Class When?

ClassTypical use
AEPrecise mounting surfaces with no machining after welding; usually needs fixtures, straightening and extra checks
BFMachine frames, welded housings, most general machine parts
CGSecondary steelwork, platforms, stairs
DHRough structures, covers and guards

A tighter class raises cost directly. Classes A and E typically need more tack welds, rigid fixtures, a balanced welding sequence, intermediate measurement and straightening where needed. If only a few surfaces are truly critical, it is usually cheaper to give them individual tolerances and leave the rest at BF or CG.

Common Mistakes

  • Applying ISO 13920 to machined surfaces: surfaces machined after welding need ISO 2768 or specific tolerances. ISO 13920 applies to the as-welded condition.
  • Using ISO 13920 for EN 1090 structures: structural steelwork tolerances are defined by the essential and functional tolerances of EN 1090-2. Mixing the two creates conflicting requirements.
  • Not stating the class letters: "ISO 13920" alone leaves the class open.
  • Forgetting the machining allowance: on parts machined after welding, the allowance must exceed the as-welded tolerance.
  • No datum surface: if it is not clear where measurement starts from, the tolerance is open to argument.

What the Workshop Does to Meet Tolerances

  • The welding sequence is planned to balance shrinkage forces.
  • Rigid fixtures or pre-setting (reverse distortion) are used for critical dimensions.
  • Long welds are split up and welded with the back-step technique.
  • Intermediate measurements are taken and deviations corrected before welding is finished.
  • Flame or mechanical straightening is applied where necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does ISO 13920-BF mean?

Class B applies to lengths and angles and class F to straightness, flatness and parallelism. It is the most common combination in general machine fabrication.

What is the difference between ISO 13920 and ISO 2768?

ISO 2768 gives general tolerances for machined parts and is much tighter. ISO 13920 is for structures in the as-welded condition. Surfaces machined after welding get ISO 2768 or specific tolerances.

Does temperature affect tolerances?

Yes. On long steel parts, temperature differences change dimensions. Critical measurements should be taken once the part is at room temperature, and the measuring temperature recorded where relevant.

What if the drawing has no tolerance?

Without a general tolerance note, the acceptance criterion between manufacturer and buyer is unclear. We recommend agreeing the class in writing at quotation stage.
