Lead Time in Welded Fabrication: How It Builds Up and How to Shorten It

2026-08-07 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The answer to “when can you deliver?” covers far more than welding time in the shop. Much of a welded part’s lead time is often spent outside welding: drawing questions, material, work done by partners, inspection and transport. Knowing these steps makes it possible to give a realistic date and shorten it when needed.

Steps That Make Up Lead Time

StepWhat drives the time
Order reviewDrawing clarity, open questions, customer approval time
Material supplyStock availability, grade (S355, S700MC, stainless), size, certificate requirements
Cutting and bendingPartner capacity, programming, part count
WeldingWeld volume, fixture, preheat, distortion control
InspectionNDT extent and method, hold points, customer witnessing
Surface treatmentPainting, galvanizing or blasting; queue at partner, curing time
Packing and transportPacking type, part or full load, customs, destination

Material: Often the Critical Path

Common grades such as S235 or S355 in plate and sections are usually available quickly. Supply takes longer for:

  • High-strength or wear-resistant special grades,
  • Z-quality (Z25, Z35) or special impact test requirements,
  • Specific stainless finishes or thicknesses,
  • A required mill or EU origin,
  • Non-standard sizes or very small quantities.

Certificate requirements matter too: material with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate is not available from every stockholder.

Work Done by Partners

If laser cutting, bending, machining, painting or galvanizing are done by partners, each has its own queue. A part travelling several times between workshop and partners (cutting, welding, machining, painting) adds up. The sequence and partner capacity should be planned at quotation.

Inspection and Hold Points

Inspection itself is often quick; the real waiting happens at hold points that need customer witnessing. If the customer or a third-party inspector cannot attend on time, production stops. Hold point dates should be planned when the order is placed. Some high-strength steels also need a waiting time before final inspection after welding (see hydrogen control).

Transport

Road transport from Konya to Central Europe takes several days; part loads add consolidation and transfer time. Customs, weekends and holidays must be counted. Under DAP, transit time is part of the seller’s lead time; under FCA or EXW, lead time ends when the goods leave the works (see Incoterms).

Typical Reasons for Longer Lead Times

  • Drawing revisions after the order,
  • Unresolved drawing questions or missing information (material grade, weld quality level, surface treatment),
  • Late customer material or approval,
  • No first article approval before series,
  • Requests for additional or repeat inspection.

How Buyers Can Shorten Lead Time

  • Start with full information: send drawing, quantity, material, quality level, inspection, surface treatment and delivery place in one go.
  • Use standard material: prefer grades and sizes that are in stock where possible.
  • Place framework orders: for repeat work, an annual quantity lets material be bought ahead and capacity reserved.
  • Accept partial deliveries: the first batch arrives early, the rest flows to plan.
  • Approve quickly: every day spent on a drawing question or first article approval adds directly to delivery.
  • Freeze revisions: if a change is needed after ordering, agree its impact in writing.

Why a Realistic Date Is Worth More

A short date that is missed disrupts the buyer’s own assembly plan. European buyers usually rate suppliers on delivery reliability. So the supplier is expected to give a date that accounts for every step, states its assumptions and flags delay risk early.

Frequently Asked Questions

What affects welded part lead time most?

Usually material supply, work done by partners (cutting, machining, surface treatment) and approval times. Welding is only part of the total.

When does lead time start?

Usually once the drawing and all required information are clear and any customer material has arrived. This definition belongs in the contract.

How can lead time be shortened?

With complete order information, standard material, framework orders, partial deliveries and quick approvals.

Is transit time included?

It depends on the delivery term. Under DAP it is; under FCA or EXW lead time ends when goods leave the works.
