What Is EN 1090 EXC3? Structural Steel CE Marking Guide

2025-12-28 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

In Europe, CE marking under EN 1090-1 is mandatory before structural steel and aluminium load-bearing components can be placed on the market. For manufacturers operating in this scope, EN 1090-2 (steel) or EN 1090-3 (aluminium) is the application standard defining technical requirements.

CE certification has become a fundamental supplier prerequisite both in domestic tender processes and in projects within the scope of exports to Europe.

Difference Between EN 1090-1 and EN 1090-2

  • EN 1090-1: The conformity assessment standard. It defines what Factory Production Control (FPC) system must be established for CE marking and which notified body to work with. The outcome is a CE DoP (Declaration of Performance).
  • EN 1090-2: The technical execution standard. Welding, NDT, tolerances, surface treatment and material requirements are defined in this standard. Compliance with EN 1090-2 also requires EN ISO 3834-2 (full quality) or EN ISO 3834-3 (standard quality) certification.

Summary: You follow the EN 1090-1 process to obtain a CE certificate. In doing so, you demonstrate compliance with EN 1090-2 technical requirements. EN ISO 3834-2 is an additional certificate independently documenting the welding quality system.

EXC Execution Classes

EN 1090-2 categorises structural components into four execution classes. The higher the class, the more demanding the requirements:

ClassApplicationNDT RateWelding Quality System
EXC1Low consequence, secondary structural componentsNone (VT sufficient)EN ISO 3834-4
EXC2Most structural steelwork in buildings, medium risk5% (NDT)EN ISO 3834-3
EXC3Bridges, heavy industrial structures, high consequence10% (critical zones 25%)EN ISO 3834-2
EXC4High seismic zones, nuclear, exceptional risk20% (critical zones 50%)EN ISO 3834-2

ArmaWeld is qualified to manufacture under EN 1090-2 EXC3. This is the most demanding commercial class covering the vast majority of the market.

What Is Required Under EXC3?

Welding Management

  • Approved WPS for all welding processes (EN ISO 15614-1/2)
  • EN ISO 9606-1/2 qualified welders
  • Welding coordinator (IWE or IWT level, EN ISO 14731)
  • No welding outside WPS scope

NDT Requirements

  • Minimum 10% of all CJP (complete joint penetration) weld seams inspected by UT or RT
  • This ratio increases to 25% at critical joints
  • EN ISO 9712 Level 2 certified NDT personnel

Material and Traceability

  • EN 10204 3.1 material certificate (3.2 in some cases)
  • Traceability of each part by HEAT/LOT number
  • Documentation of welding procedure, welder and material chain

CE Marking Process

Steps required for a manufacturer to obtain CE certification under EN 1090-1:

  1. Establishing the Factory Production Control (FPC) System: Procedures, records, equipment calibration system and personnel competencies are documented.
  2. Notified Body Application: An EU-accredited notified body (e.g. TÜV, Bureau Veritas, DNV) conducts a factory audit.
  3. Initial Type Testing: Representative product samples are tested against standard requirements.
  4. CE DoP Issue: The declaration of conformity is published and the CE mark is affixed to the product.
  5. Annual Surveillance Audit: The notified body audits the factory once a year.

Note for EXC3: The notified body must explicitly approve the execution class. EXC3 projects cannot be undertaken with an EXC2 certificate.

Relationship with EN ISO 3834-2

The EN ISO 3834-2 (Comprehensive Quality) certificate, mandatory for EN 1090 EXC3, demonstrates that the welding quality management system has been approved by an independent certification body. This certificate:

  • Is valid not only for EN 1090-scope work but for all welded fabrication projects
  • Complements ISO 9001; covers welding process-specific requirements
  • Also intersects with other standards such as PED (Pressure Equipment Directive) and EN 15085 (railway)
