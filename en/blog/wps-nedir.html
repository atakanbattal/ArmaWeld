What Is WPS? A Guide to Welding Procedure Specification

2024-07-30 9 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A Welding Procedure Specification (WPS) is the technical document that defines how a particular weld joint is to be produced. The parameters it contains — process, material group, thickness range, welding position, filler metal, shielding gas, preheat and interpass temperature — form the framework to which the welder must adhere during production.

A WPS is mandatory in every production carried out under EN ISO 3834-2 and EN 1090 EXC2/3/4. Welding with an unapproved WPS can invalidate the structure's certification and CE marking.

Mandatory Basis of a WPS

The WPS requirement originates from multiple standards:

  • EN ISO 3834-2 — Under comprehensive quality requirements, an approved WPS is mandatory.
  • EN 1090-2 — A WPS is required for every welding process in execution classes EXC2 and above.
  • EN 13445 — No welding may be performed on pressure vessels without a WPS + WPQR pair.
  • PED 2014/68/EU — Welding procedure approval is mandatory for pressure equipment Category II and above.

A WPS is therefore not merely a rule; it is the technical foundation that proves the reliability of the structure.

Relationship with WPQR

A WPS must be based on a WPQR (Welding Procedure Qualification Record), an experimentally validated document. The sequence is:

  1. pWPS (preliminary WPS) is prepared — an engineer's draft based on technical knowledge.
  2. Qualification weld is made — with destructive test specimens.
  3. Destructive tests are performed — tensile, bend, hardness, Charpy, macro.
  4. WPQR is prepared — signed by an independent approving body (NB or accredited lab).
  5. WPS is approved referencing the WPQR and used in production.

Important: The WPS qualification range is derived from the test conditions in the WPQR. For example, a WPQR made on 12 mm may cover a range of 6–24 mm; however, it does not cover 25 mm.
