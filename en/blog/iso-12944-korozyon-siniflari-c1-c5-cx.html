ISO 12944 Corrosivity Categories C1–C5 and CX: Choosing a Coating System for Welded Steel

2026-03-25 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

If the coating of a steel structure is not chosen for where the part will be used, it is either more expensive than necessary or rusts within a few years. ISO 12944 creates a common language for protecting steel structures with paint systems: it defines the aggressiveness of the environment with a corrosivity category and how long the coating must last with a durability range.

Parts of the Standard

  • Part 2: Classification of environments (C1–CX, Im1–Im4)
  • Part 3: Design considerations (accessibility, gaps, edges)
  • Part 4: Types of surface and surface preparation
  • Part 5: Protective paint systems
  • Parts 6 and 9: Laboratory performance tests (9: offshore and CX)
  • Parts 7 and 8: Execution, supervision and developing specifications

Atmospheric Corrosivity Categories

The category is defined by the first-year thickness loss of low-carbon steel. Typical environments are also shown:

CategoryCorrosivityFirst-year thickness loss (steel)Typical environment
C1Very low≤ 1.3 µmHeated dry interiors, offices
C2Low1.3–25 µmUnheated storage, rural atmosphere
C3Medium25–50 µmUrban and light industrial atmosphere, humid production halls
C4High50–80 µmIndustrial areas, coastal, chemical plants
C5Very high80–200 µmAggressive industrial, high humidity, coastal
CXExtreme200–700 µmOffshore, tropical with salt spray

For structures immersed in water or buried in soil, categories Im1 (fresh water), Im2 (sea or brackish water), Im3 (soil) and Im4 (sea water with cathodic protection) apply.

Durability

In ISO 12944, durability is the time to the first major maintenance; it is not a guarantee period. Ranges in the 2018 edition:

  • L (low): up to 7 years
  • M (medium): 7–15 years
  • H (high): 15–25 years
  • VH (very high): more than 25 years

Specifications therefore state both, for example “C4-H”: high corrosivity, 15–25 years durability.

How to Choose a Coating System

ISO 12944-5 gives example systems by corrosivity category and durability, with primer, intermediate and topcoat types and total dry film thickness (DFT). In practice, selection is made from these tables and the paint manufacturer’s approved system sheet for that category. General trends:

  • Higher category and durability mean higher total DFT and more coats.
  • Zinc-rich primers are common from C4 upwards.
  • In very severe conditions, hot-dip galvanizing plus paint (duplex system) may be preferred (see designing welded parts for galvanizing).

What to Watch in Welded Fabrication

Coating performance depends largely on the work done before painting:

  • Surface preparation: the ISO 8501-3 P grade suited to the category (see P1, P2, P3) and blast cleanliness to ISO 8501-1, usually Sa 2½.
  • Edges: paint thins on sharp edges; in higher categories edges are rounded and an extra stripe coat is applied.
  • Design: ISO 12944-3 recommends avoiding narrow gaps the brush or gun cannot reach, water traps and intermittent welds. Drain holes and sealed welds on closed sections matter.
  • Weld: spatter, porosity and undercut are weak points of the coating; weld quality directly affects coating life.

What the Specification Should State

For correct quotation and fabrication, the following should be clear: corrosivity category and durability, P preparation grade, blast grade and roughness, coating system (product, number of coats, DFT), colour (RAL), surfaces not to be painted (bolted contact surfaces, site weld areas) and inspection method (DFT measurement, adhesion test).

Frequently Asked Questions

What does ISO 12944 C4 mean?

A high-corrosivity atmosphere in which low-carbon steel loses 50–80 µm in the first year; industrial and coastal areas are typical.

What does C4-H mean?

A coating system for high corrosivity (C4) targeting a durability of 15–25 years (H).

Is durability a guarantee?

No. In ISO 12944 durability is the technical time to first major maintenance; a guarantee is a separate contractual matter.

What should be done to welds before painting?

Preparation to the ISO 8501-3 P grade suited to the category (removing spatter, pores, sharp edges), followed usually by Sa 2½ blasting.
