Architectural Steel and Exposed Welds: Appearance Grades, Mock-up Approval and Grinding

2026-08-24 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A single-stringer stair in a hotel lobby, slender columns on a shop front, a canopy at a campus entrance: in architectural steel, the weld is both structural and visual. Most disputes in this work come from undefined wishes like "make it look nice". Good sourcing starts by making appearance measurable.

Define the Appearance

Some countries use categories for exposed steel (such as AESS classes); in European projects requirements are often written item by item in the specification. Define:

  • Which surfaces are visible, and from what distance?
  • Are welds ground flush, or left as neat beads?
  • Are joint gaps and bolt heads visible or concealed?
  • Are mill marks, cut edges and marking traces acceptable?

Mock-up Approval

Before series work, a mock-up of a typical joint or short piece approved by the architect and client is the step that prevents most disputes. The approved mock-up is kept as the reference throughout fabrication.

Welding Workmanship

  • Ground welds: grinding must not reduce the section; where full penetration is needed, fill and grind are planned. Grinding can affect capacity, so engineer approval is needed.
  • Continuous welds: gaps between intermittent welds bleed rust under paint; use continuous welds in visible areas.
  • Distortion control: ripples on slender sections catch the eye; prevent them with sequence and fixtures.

Surface and Coating

Edge rounding and spatter removal decide coating durability (see ISO 8501-3 preparation grades). With weathering steel the filler must also be weathering grade (see welding weathering steel). Powder versus liquid coating is weighed for colour, gloss and repairability.

Packing and Erection

Painted architectural parts must be protected against scratches in transport and erection: corner protectors, spacers and planned lifting points. Supply touch-up paint from the same batch.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes most disputes?

Undefined appearance expectations; write them item by item in the specification.

Why a mock-up?

The approved mock-up becomes the acceptance reference.

Can welds be ground?

Yes, without reducing the section; structural welds need engineer approval.

How are painted parts protected?

Corner protectors, spacers and planned lifting points; send touch-up paint.
