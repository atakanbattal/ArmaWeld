What Is Welded Manufacturing? Core Processes and Standards

2026-04-22 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Welded manufacturing is the production method in which metal parts are permanently joined using heat and/or pressure, usually with a filler material, to form a final product or structure. Chassis, pressure vessels, structural steel, defense platforms and agricultural machinery are produced using welded manufacturing. In Konya and across Türkiye’s industrial hubs, welded manufacturing is a discipline where engineering design, material selection, production process and quality control are fully integrated.

This article examines the core steps of welded manufacturing, the most common welding processes, quality requirements and the EN ISO 3834-2 and EN 1090 standards from a hands-on perspective.

1. Core Steps of Welded Manufacturing

A welded manufacturing project is far more than the welding step alone. From design inputs to final delivery, multiple stages are managed under tight discipline.

  • Engineering design: Load analysis, material selection, weld details and tolerances are defined.
  • Material procurement: Steel plate, sections and consumables with EN 10204 3.1 certificates are sourced.
  • Cutting and forming: Laser/plasma cutting, press brake forming and machining.
  • Pre-weld preparation: Joint preparation, cleaning, preheating and tacking.
  • Welding: Performed by qualified welders following an approved WPS.
  • Inspection: Visual, PT, MT, UT or RT NDT.
  • Surface finishing & shipment: Blasting, painting or galvanizing followed by delivery.

3. Quality and Traceability Standards

In professional welded manufacturing, every weld must be traceable. EN ISO 3834-2 requires assigning a welding coordinator, managing WPS/WPQR, controlled consumable storage and inspection records. For structural steel, EN 1090-2 applies and quality is set per EXC1–EXC4 execution classes.
