Carbon Footprint and EPDs for Welded Parts: What European Customers Ask For

2026-11-13 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

European machine and equipment makers increasingly ask their suppliers for carbon data. Sometimes it is a questionnaire, sometimes a "kg CO₂e" value per order, sometimes a verified environmental product declaration (EPD). For a welded parts supplier the questions are: which data is wanted, where does it come from, and what can realistically be provided?

Why Customers Ask

  • Corporate reporting: large companies in the EU address supply chain emissions (Scope 3) in sustainability reporting.
  • Product declarations: in construction products and some machinery markets, customers ask for product-level EPDs.
  • Tender scoring: low-carbon products can be a criterion in public and large private purchasing.
  • CBAM: certain steel products imported into the EU require reporting of embedded emissions (see CBAM and steel exports).

Key Terms

TermMeaning
Scope 1The company's own direct emissions (e.g. heating and vehicle fuel)
Scope 2Indirect emissions from purchased electricity and heat
Scope 3Other indirect emissions in the supply chain; from the customer's view the welded parts supplier sits here
Product carbon footprint (PCF)CO₂ equivalent for a product's life cycle or defined stages; based on ISO 14067
EPDIndependently verified environmental product declaration under ISO 14025; EN 15804 for construction products
Cradle-to-gateScope from raw material to factory gate; the most common scope for supplier data

The table shows that the biggest lever on a welded part's footprint is the steel itself and how much of it ends up as scrap.

Effect of the Steelmaking Route

Steel is made by two main routes: from iron ore via blast furnace and basic oxygen furnace (BF-BOF), or mainly from scrap in an electric arc furnace (EAF). Scrap-based EAF steel usually has markedly lower emissions per tonne, but the exact value depends on the producer, the scrap share and the electricity source. Calculations should therefore use the steel producer's own EPD or declared value instead of a generic average whenever possible.

Data Requested from Suppliers

  • Net and gross steel weight per part (including scrap),
  • Steel producer, grade and heat; material certificate (see EN 10204 3.1),
  • The steel producer's EPD or carbon intensity declaration if available,
  • Workshop electricity use and source (grid, renewable contract, rooftop solar, etc.),
  • Welding consumables and gas use,
  • Galvanising or paint information (from the partner),
  • Transport distance and mode.

Most of this can already be taken from well-kept production records. The real work is collecting it consistently per order.

PCF or EPD?

Not every customer needs a verified EPD. In many cases a product carbon footprint calculated with a recognised method and clearly stated assumptions is enough. An EPD requires a programme operator, product category rules (PCR) and independent verification; it mostly makes sense for standard product families and construction products. Asking the customer which is needed before ordering avoids unnecessary cost.

Practical Ways to Reduce Emissions

  • Material efficiency: good nesting, design that reduces scrap and alignment with standard plate sizes.
  • Design optimisation: plate thicker than needed and oversized welds cost both money and carbon.
  • Low-carbon steel: EAF steel or low-carbon product options where available.
  • Right first time: repairs, rework and scrapped parts all add emissions.
  • Nearby sourcing and transport planning: full loads, suitable mode and shorter routes (see Turkey vs Eastern Europe sourcing).
  • Surface treatment choice: a protection system that extends service life reduces early renewal.

Honest Data

The biggest risk in carbon data is figures of unclear origin. It should be stated clearly which values were measured, which came from generic databases and what assumptions were made. For a report that will be audited on the customer's side, a transparent approximate value is worth more than a precise-looking figure whose source cannot be explained.

Frequently Asked Questions

What affects a welded part's carbon footprint most?

Usually the steel itself and the scrap rate; the steelmaking route changes the value considerably.

What is the difference between an EPD and a PCF?

A PCF is a calculation (ISO 14067); an EPD is a declaration prepared under ISO 14025 and independently verified.

Why do customers ask suppliers for carbon data?

To report their supply chain emissions (Scope 3), prepare product declarations or meet obligations such as CBAM.

Is EAF steel always lower-carbon?

Usually markedly lower, but it depends on scrap share and electricity source; the producer's declaration should be used.
