How to Read a Weld Inspection Report: The Fields a Buyer Should Check

2025-12-11 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The inspection reports that arrive with a welded part are the written proof that it was made to specification. Yet many purchasing and quality teams file them after checking only whether they say "accepted". This article explains which fields to look at to judge whether a report actually proves anything.

What Every Inspection Report Should Contain

Whatever the method, a traceable report should include:

FieldWhy it matters
Part, drawing and revision numberTies the report to a specific part
Weld or seam numberLinks to the weld map
Testing standardDefines how the test was done (e.g. ISO 17637, ISO 17640)
Acceptance standard and levelDefines which indications are acceptable (e.g. ISO 5817 B, ISO 11666 level 2)
Extent of testingWhat percentage of the weld was tested
Time of testingHow long after welding (critical for steels at risk of delayed cracking)
Equipment and calibrationInstrument serial number, calibration date
Indications and positionsDiscontinuities found, their size and location
ResultAccept or reject, per weld
Personnel and levelWho tested and who approved, ISO 9712 level

If any of these is missing, the report may cause trouble in an audit or a failure investigation.

Testing Standard vs Acceptance Standard

This is the point most often confused. The testing standard says how the test is performed, the acceptance standard says how the result is judged.

MethodTesting standardTypical acceptance standard
Visual (VT)EN ISO 17637EN ISO 5817
Penetrant (PT)EN ISO 3452-1EN ISO 23277
Magnetic particle (MT)EN ISO 17638EN ISO 23278
Ultrasonic (UT)EN ISO 17640EN ISO 11666
Radiography (RT)EN ISO 17636-1EN ISO 10675-1

The acceptance level must also match the ISO 5817 quality level on the drawing. For ISO 5817 B, UT usually uses ISO 11666 acceptance level 2 and RT uses ISO 10675-1 level 1. ISO 17635 describes this correlation.

The VT Report

Visual testing is done on every weld and is a precondition for all other methods. A good VT report shows:

  • Measured throat thickness or leg length on fillet welds
  • Cap height, undercut and misalignment on butt welds
  • Surface porosity, cracks, spatter and arc strikes
  • The gauge used and lighting conditions

A one-line note saying "visually checked, OK" is not a VT report.

PT and MT Reports

These surface-crack methods should record the type and batch of consumables (penetrant, developer or magnetic ink), surface preparation, dwell time and, for MT, the magnetisation technique. MT only works on ferromagnetic materials; austenitic stainless steel needs PT.

The UT Report

UT reports are more technical. Check:

  • Probe type, angle and frequency
  • Reference block and calibration method (DAC, DGS or TCG)
  • Testing level (A, B, C or D to ISO 17640)
  • Position, length and amplitude of recorded indications
  • Assessment of each indication against the acceptance level

A UT report that says "no defects found" without stating scan coverage and recording threshold leaves unclear what it covers.

The RT Report

An RT report should list the film or image number, source type (X-ray or isotope), image quality indicator (IQI) value, film density and the indications on each film. The film archive is part of the report and should be available to the buyer on request.

Extent of Testing: What Does 10% Mean?

"10% UT" in a specification can mean 10% of each weld or 10% of the welds. The contract should say which. EN 1090-2 defines supplementary NDT rates by execution class and weld type, and the standard also sets rules for extending the scope when a sampled area fails.

What Should a Buyer Check?

  1. Do the part and revision numbers match the delivered part?
  2. Does the acceptance level match the quality level on the drawing?
  3. Is the extent equal to the specified rate?
  4. Is the inspector's ISO 9712 certificate valid and does it cover the method?
  5. If welds were rejected, are the repair and re-test reports attached?
  6. For steels needing a hold time, is the time of testing stated?

Frequently Asked Questions

Who should sign an inspection report?

The person evaluating the result and approving the report should hold ISO 9712 Level 2 or Level 3 in the method. Level 1 personnel may perform the test but cannot evaluate the result on their own.

What is the difference between recording level and acceptance level?

The recording level defines the smallest indication that must be noted. The acceptance level defines which indications cause rejection. A recorded indication is not automatically a reject.

How long should inspection reports be kept?

It depends on contract, product standard and sector. EN 1090 and pressure equipment projects usually require ten years or more. We recommend stating the period in the contract.

Are digital reports accepted?

Yes, as long as it is traceable who approved them and when. If wet signatures are required, this should be stated in the contract.
