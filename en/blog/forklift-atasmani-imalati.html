Forklift Attachment Manufacturing: What to Settle Before Approaching a Supplier

2026-03-30 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Forklift attachments (buckets, fork extensions, coil rams, jibs, drum handlers, tipping devices) are often designed for a company's own needs and built in small quantities. Simple as they look, they directly affect the truck's capacity, stability and operator safety. Settling the points below before requesting a quote shapes both the price and the result in the field.

Truck-Side Information

  • Carriage class: for hook-on attachments, the carriage plate size follows ISO 2328 classes (Class II, III, IV and so on). The wrong class means the attachment simply won't fit.
  • Fork section and length: for fork-mounted attachments (extensions, buckets), the fork dimensions and spacing.
  • Truck capacity and load centre: an attachment reduces usable capacity through its own weight and by moving the load centre forward. This must be calculated with the truck manufacturer's capacity chart.
  • Hydraulics: if there is an extra hydraulic function, pressure, flow and connection type.

Manufacturing Topics

  • Material: main load-bearing parts are usually S355; wear surfaces (bucket lip, blade) use wear plate (see wear plate welding).
  • Fork pockets and guides: the inner size and parallelism of fork pockets are affected by distortion, so a fixture or a set weld sequence is needed.
  • Retention: a pin, chain or locking device that stops the attachment sliding off the forks.
  • Welding: carriage hooks and fork pocket connections are load-bearing welds; weld type and size must be on the drawing.
  • Surface: blasting and painting; for outdoor use, a system suited to the corrosivity category.

Data Plate and Documents

Attachments placed on the EU market as interchangeable equipment fall under the Machinery Regulation and need CE marking, a declaration of conformity and instructions (see new Machinery Regulation). The attachment should carry a plate with manufacturer, serial number, own weight, capacity and load centre. CE and design responsibility usually lie with the company placing the attachment on the market; the welding shop supplies the manufacturing records.

What to Send for a Quote

  • Assembly and part drawings (STEP + PDF).
  • Truck model, carriage class or fork size.
  • Capacity and load centre target.
  • Quantity, paint colour, plate content.
  • Test expectations (for example whether a load test is needed).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is ISO 2328?

The standard that defines carriage plate dimensions for hook-on forklift attachments by class.

Does an attachment reduce truck capacity?

Yes; its weight and the forward shift of the load centre reduce usable capacity.

Does a forklift attachment need CE?

In the EU, if placed on the market as interchangeable equipment, it falls under the Machinery Regulation.

What should the data plate show?

Manufacturer, serial number, own weight, capacity and load centre.
