Machining After Welding: Bringing Welded Parts to Precision Dimensions

2026-10-07 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Even with the best fixtures and weld sequence, welding can leave a few millimetres of distortion. Yet bearing bores, seating faces and mounting flanges often need tolerances in hundredths of a millimetre. The way to join these two worlds is to machine the precision features after welding. Planned badly, the allowance is too small, datums can’t be found, or the machined part changes shape a few days later.

What Should Be Machined After Welding?

  • Bearing and pin bores, especially those whose axial alignment matters,
  • Bearing, slide and rail seating faces,
  • Flanges and end faces where other machine elements attach,
  • Tapped holes where position is critical,
  • Base faces with tight flatness tolerance.

The rule is simple: any dimension whose tolerance is tighter than the expected weld distortion should be machined after welding.

Machining Allowance

The allowance depends on part size, amount of welding and expected distortion. Large, heavily welded parts need more. Too little leaves “black” (unmachined) patches on the face; too much adds time and cost. On thick plates, the allowance should be considered when choosing plate thickness.

Datum Strategy

  • Main datum faces and axes for machining should be defined on the drawing.
  • The welding shop can transfer centrelines or datum marks to the part.
  • On the machine the part is aligned so that every face has enough allowance (allowance distribution).
  • Related dimensions should be machined in one set-up where possible.

Stress Relief

Residual welding stresses redistribute as material is removed. Result: a part that is on size on the machine changes shape when unclamped or a few days later. For precision parts, thermal stress relief before machining reduces this risk. Releasing the part after rough machining and then finish machining is also common practice.

Clamping

Welded parts are usually irregular and can flex when clamped. If clamped under strain, the part springs back after release and goes out of tolerance. Supports should take the part without forcing it, and clamping forces should act on stiff areas.

Heat-Affected Zone and Machinability

The weld and heat-affected zone can be harder than the base metal. Especially with high-strength and wear-resistant steels, this increases tool wear. Where possible, design machined faces away from welds; otherwise choose cutting parameters accordingly.

Welds Under Machined Faces

If a weld lies just beneath a machined face, machining can expose porosity or lack of fusion. Such welds should be held to a higher quality level, or positioned away from the machined zone.

No Welding After Machining

All welds, including small brackets and lifting lugs, should be completed before machining. Welding after machining can spoil machined dimensions. If unavoidable, keep the weld away from machined faces and protect them.

Blasting, Painting and Protection

  • Blasting and priming are usually done before machining; machined faces are then masked for the topcoat.
  • Apply rust preventive to machined faces and plug holes.
  • For galvanised parts, plan in advance how machined faces will be protected or re-machined after galvanising.

Measurement

On large parts, machined dimensions are checked with a measuring arm, laser tracker or coordinate measuring machine and recorded in a dimensional report. Showing welded general dimensions and machined dimensions separately in the report makes evaluation easier.

The Right Drawing Notes

  • Machining and surface roughness symbols for machined faces,
  • A “machine after welding” note and allowance where needed,
  • ISO 13920 class for welded dimensions and separate tolerances for machined ones (see ISO 13920),
  • Geometric tolerances and datums (see dimensional tolerances),
  • Stress relief requirement.

Single Responsibility

When welding and machining are done by different companies, an out-of-tolerance part can lead to arguments over responsibility. If the welding shop coordinates machining and delivers the part machined with a dimensional report, the buyer has one contact and clear responsibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which dimensions should be machined after welding?

Any dimension tighter than weld distortion: bearing and pin bores, seating faces, mounting flanges and bases with tight flatness.

Why does a machined part change shape later?

Residual welding stresses redistribute as material is removed. Stress relief and releasing the part between rough and finish machining reduce this risk.

Can you weld after machining?

Not recommended; it can spoil machined dimensions. All welds should be completed before machining.

Should welding and machining come from one supplier?

A single contact makes dimensional responsibility clear; the welding shop can coordinate machining and deliver the part machined.
