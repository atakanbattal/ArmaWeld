Crane Booms and Lifting Equipment Fabrication: What Does EN 13001 Require?

2026-03-10 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Crane booms, bridge crane girders, telescopic arms and lifting beams work under constantly changing loads. In these structures weld quality determines service life as much as design does. In Europe cranes are designed to the EN 13001 series; product-specific standards (for example EN 15011 for bridge cranes and EN 12999 for loader cranes) build on it. For a welded parts supplier, what matters is that the weld quality assumed in design is actually delivered in fabrication.

EN 13001 and Welded Structures

EN 13001-3-1 covers limit states and proof of competence of steel structures. The fatigue strength of welded details is calculated assuming certain detail categories and weld quality levels. Drawings or technical specifications therefore usually define:

  • The ISO 5817 quality level of the welds (often B or C, sometimes with additional requirements) (see ISO 5817 quality levels).
  • Which welds need full penetration and root treatment.
  • Toe grinding or smooth transitions in fatigue-critical areas (see fatigue and FAT classes).
  • NDT methods and inspection rates.

The fabricator must understand these requirements and select welding procedures accordingly. A misunderstood quality level means the calculated life will not be reached in the field.

Material Selection

In lifting equipment, weight is directly lost capacity. That is why high-strength steels such as S690QL, S700MC or S960QL are common in booms and arms. With these steels, the heat input window, preheat, interpass temperature and choice of consumable are critical; excessive heat input reduces strength and toughness in the heat-affected zone (see S960QL welding parameters, S700MC and t8/5). For equipment working at low temperatures, toughness requirements also apply.

Long Welds and Distortion

In box-section booms the web-to-flange welds are metres long. Mechanised welding (for example submerged arc) helps achieve consistent penetration and weld geometry (see submerged arc for booms and frames). Weld sequence and fixturing determine straightness, twist and camber. Dimensions must be checked and reported at several points along the length.

Inspection and Documentation

  • Visual inspection of all welds (see ISO 17637).
  • UT or RT on full-penetration butt welds, MT on fillet and T-joints.
  • Material certificates (3.1, or 3.2 where required) and part traceability.
  • Welder and procedure qualifications, weld map.
  • Dimensional report: straightness, twist, pin hole positions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does EN 13001 require for weld quality?

The detail categories and quality levels assumed in design (usually ISO 5817 B or C, with extra requirements if needed) must be achieved in fabrication.

Which steels are used in crane booms?

Often high-strength steels such as S690QL, S700MC or S960QL to save weight.

Which NDT methods are used on crane booms?

Visual inspection on all welds; UT or RT on full-penetration butt welds; MT on fillet and T-joints.

How is distortion controlled on long booms?

Through weld sequence, fixturing, mechanised welding and multi-point dimensional checks along the length.
