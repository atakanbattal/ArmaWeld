Industrial Furnace Casing Fabrication: Steel Shell, Refractory Anchors and Heat-Resistant Parts

2026-08-17 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Builders of heat treatment, tempering, drying, paint curing and melting furnaces often have the steel frame and sheet casing made by a welding subcontractor, keeping combustion, controls and refractory as their own expertise. The casing may look like a simple box, but temperature, expansion and tightness need attention.

Parts of the Casing

  • Outer shell and frame: usually carbon steel sections and sheet; it carries refractory and insulation and stays cool outside.
  • Refractory anchors: V, Y or special anchors holding castable or ceramic fibre; quantity and pattern come from the refractory design.
  • Doors and frames: rigid frames resistant to thermal distortion; flatness of sealing faces matters.
  • Internals: muffles, radiant tube supports, hearth grids and baskets are usually in heat-resistant stainless.

Thermal Expansion

Expansion differences between hot and cold zones stress welded joints and cause cracks. Designs include sliding joints, expansion gaps and flexible connections; the fabricator must not "fix" these by welding them shut. Points marked "do not weld" on the drawing must stay that way.

Tightness

Controlled-atmosphere furnaces need gas-tight casing seams. Plan continuous welds, proper starts and stops, and a leak test where required. Even without atmosphere, hot gas leaks mean lost efficiency and hot spots outside.

Welding Heat-Resistant Grades

High chromium-nickel heat-resistant stainless steels need suitable filler and heat input control. Where stainless internals join the carbon steel shell, 309-type fillers are common for the dissimilar joint (see stainless to carbon steel welding).

Dimensions and Distortion

Large panels ripple under welding heat; stiffener layout and welding sequence limit distortion (see welding distortion). Mounting faces such as door frames and burner flanges are machined after welding where needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a furnace casing made of?

Usually carbon steel outside; hot-zone internals in heat-resistant stainless.

How is thermal expansion handled?

With sliding joints and expansion gaps, which must not be welded shut.

Must the casing be gas tight?

For controlled atmosphere, yes; continuous welds and testing where required.

Which filler for dissimilar joints?

Often 309-type between stainless and carbon steel.
