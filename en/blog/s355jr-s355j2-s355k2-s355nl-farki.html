S355JR vs S355J2 vs S355K2 vs S355NL: Which S355 Grade Should You Choose?

2026-04-01 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

S355 is the most widely used structural steel in welded fabrication. But “S355” on a drawing is not enough: the letters for toughness quality determine how the part behaves against brittle fracture in the cold. The wrong grade either adds unnecessary cost or creates risk at low temperature.

What the Designation Says

S means structural steel, 355 is the minimum yield strength (MPa) in the thinnest range. The letters that follow state the temperature and energy for the Charpy V-notch impact test.

Yield Strength Falls with Thickness

The “355” applies to thin plate. Per EN 10025-2, typical minimum yield for S355 is 355 MPa up to 16 mm, 345 MPa for 16–40 mm and 335 MPa for 40–63 mm. Tensile strength is 470–630 MPa for 3–100 mm. Design of thick parts must account for this reduction.

Weldability: CEV

For welding, the carbon equivalent (CEV) matters. EN 10025-2 limits CEV for S355 to about 0.45–0.47 depending on thickness, and a lower CEV can be ordered. Higher CEV and thickness create a need for preheat (see preheat calculation), so the actual analysis on the certificate is important.

Delivery Condition: +AR and +N

EN 10025-2 grades can be delivered as rolled (+AR) or normalized rolled (+N). S355J2+N gives a more uniform grain structure and more reliable toughness, preferred especially for parts that will be heat treated or hot formed later. N and NL grades belong to a separate standard, EN 10025-3, in the normalized condition.

Which Grade When?

  • S355JR: indoor, thin and medium parts with no low-temperature or dynamic-load risk.
  • S355J2: the most common choice for general steel structures and machine fabrication; outdoor and medium thickness.
  • S355K2: thick parts, high restraint, dynamic loading or colder climate.
  • S355NL / ML: toughness down to −50 °C, e.g. Nordic countries or cold storage.

The exact choice is made with the EN 1993-1-10 table: for the lowest service temperature, stress level and plate thickness, the maximum permitted thickness for each grade is read. At the same temperature, J2 allows considerably greater thickness than JR.

Certificate Check

At goods receipt, the EN 10204 3.1 certificate should be checked for grade, delivery condition, chemical analysis, CEV, tensile and impact results (see EN 10204 3.1). Thick T joints may also need a Z grade (see lamellar tearing).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between S355JR and S355J2?

Yield strength is the same; the difference is toughness. JR meets 27 J at +20 °C, J2 at −20 °C, so J2 is safer against brittle fracture in the cold.

When is S355K2 chosen?

For thick parts, high restraint, dynamic loads or cold climates. K2 offers 40 J at −20 °C, higher toughness than J2.

What is S355NL?

A normalized S355 grade to EN 10025-3 meeting 27 J at −50 °C, for low-temperature applications.

What does S355J2+N mean?

S355J2 delivered normalized or normalized rolled, giving a more uniform structure and reliable toughness.
