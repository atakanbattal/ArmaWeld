Chute, Hopper and Wear Liner Fabrication: Replaceable Design and Correct Welding

2026-05-29 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Transfer chutes between conveyors, crusher and screen feed hoppers, truck dump hoppers: every bulk handling plant has these welded parts, and they all face the same problem, wear. The chute body may last for years while the liner inside can be gone in months. A good chute is therefore designed and built so that the wearing part can be replaced easily.

Body and Liner

  • Load-bearing body: usually S235/S355 structural steel; carries load and holds the liner.
  • Wear liner: wear-resistant steel plate, clad plate, ceramic or rubber on impact and sliding surfaces.
  • Impact zone: where material lands; may need thick plate or impact bars (rock box).

Bolted or Welded Liners?

Bolted liners (countersunk bolts) allow fast site replacement without welding on the body, but holes and bolt heads are exposed to wear. Welded liners are cheaper and give a smooth surface, but replacement needs grinding and site welding. Bolted liners are common where replacement is frequent, welded ones where it is rare. Liner pieces should be sized to a weight that can be handled manually or with a small hoist.

Welding Wear Plate

High-hardness wear plates need preheat, low-hydrogen consumables and controlled heat input, or cracking may occur (see wear plate welding). A lower-strength, ductile filler is often used. Cutting is affected by heat too; method and edge hardness must be considered.

Design Tips

  • Slopes that prevent material striking the liner head-on and material-on-material (dead box) solutions extend liner life.
  • Inspection doors and access openings should be planned for liner change.
  • Liner pieces should be numbered and standardised so a spare set can be stocked.
  • Seals on doors and flanges should be considered for dust tightness.

Repair or Replace?

Worn bodies can be built up or patched by welding; if the body has thinned, replacement can be more economical (see weld repair). Building a new chute to match an existing one requires site measurement or reverse engineering of the old part.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should chute liners be bolted or welded?

Bolted where replacement is frequent, welded where it is rare.

What matters when welding wear plate?

Preheat, low-hydrogen consumables, controlled heat input and usually a ductile filler.

How can liner life be extended?

With slopes that avoid head-on impact and material-on-material (dead box) designs.

Can a worn chute be repaired?

If the body is thick enough, build-up or patching works; if thinned, replacement may be cheaper.
