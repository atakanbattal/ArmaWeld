Sourcing Welded Parts for the Benelux: Netherlands, Belgium and Luxembourg

2026-02-16 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The Benelux countries have a dense industry in machine building, food and agricultural technology, logistics equipment, energy and steel construction. In the Netherlands and Belgium, machine builders buy a large share of their welded parts externally; Luxembourg is a small but long-established market for steel and industrial equipment. All three are EU members, but they differ in language, ways of doing business and logistics.

Three Countries, Three Different Tones

CountryTechnical languagePractical note
NetherlandsDutch, English widely usedDirect, fast communication; open negotiation on price and cost
BelgiumDutch in Flanders, French in Wallonia; English commonChoosing the language by the contact's region matters
LuxembourgFrench and German; English commonSmall market where relationships and reliability come first

In practice, technical documents and correspondence usually run in English. Still, a quotation cover or short summary in the contact's language makes a good impression.

Technical Expectations

  • EN 1090: CE marking and factory production control for load-bearing steel components (see EN 1090-1 FPC).
  • EN ISO 3834: frequently requested for welding quality management in machine and equipment building.
  • Documents: welder certificates, WPS/WPQR, EN 10204 3.1 material certificates, dimensional and NDT reports.
  • Surface: suitable ISO 12944 corrosivity category and paint system in coastal and industrial areas; stainless steel and hygienic welding for food and agricultural equipment (see hygienic stainless welding).

Customs and Documents

As all three countries are EU members, industrial goods shipped from Turkey use the A.TR movement certificate (see A.TR and origin). Import VAT and the customs declaration are usually handled by the buyer or their representative; that responsibility is set by the delivery term.

Logistics

Welded parts from Turkey to the Benelux can travel by road as full or part truckloads, or by sea through ports such as Rotterdam and Antwerp. Sea freight may suit long, bulky parts, road freight urgent jobs. Packaging, loading plan and delivery term should be clarified before ordering (see packaging and Incoterms).

Questions Benelux Buyers Ask

  • What is the total cost (part, transport, customs, quality risk)?
  • Is the lead time realistic, and how will it be tracked?
  • If something goes wrong, who responds and how quickly?
  • How can quality records be accessed?

A supplier who can answer these clearly is in a stronger position than one competing on unit price alone (see nearshoring and Turkey).
