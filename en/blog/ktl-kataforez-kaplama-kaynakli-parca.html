Cathodic E-Coating (KTL) and Welded Parts: Slag, Lap Joints and Drainage

2026-09-10 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

In automotive, agricultural and construction machinery supply chains, many welded parts are delivered cathodic e-coated (KTL). After pretreatment the part is immersed in a paint bath and an electric current deposits an even film on all surfaces, edges and reachable cavities. That excellent throwing power also exposes welding defects mercilessly.

The Process in Brief

  • Pretreatment: degreasing, rinsing and usually zinc phosphate conversion coating.
  • E-coat bath: the part is the cathode; paint particles deposit on the surface.
  • Curing: oven baking forms a thin, hard, corrosion-resistant film.

Welding-Related Problems

  • Silicate slag: the glassy silicate islands on MAG welds are non-conductive; e-coat does not cover them and rust starts quickly. Remove slag by blasting, brushing or grinding.
  • Spatter: loose spatter stays under the coating and later falls off, leaving bare spots.
  • Lap and intermittent joints: tight gaps between plates hold pretreatment fluids, which seep out in the oven causing stains, blisters and rust traces. Decide in design whether laps are seal welded or left fully open.
  • Closed sections: need drain and vent holes so bath fluid can enter and leave; otherwise the inside stays uncoated and fluid collects.

Hanging, Contact and Masking

Current reaches the part through hanging and contact points, which stay uncoated and must be placed sensibly. Threads, bearing seats and earthing faces are masked or cleaned afterwards.

E-Coat Alone or with Powder?

Epoxy e-coat chalks in sunlight over time. For visible and outdoor parts it is usually a primer under a powder topcoat (see powder or wet paint). Under-chassis and hidden parts may need e-coat only. Part size is limited by the tank.

What to Specify

E-coat specification and film thickness, how weld slag is removed, positions of drain and hanging holes, surfaces to mask and whether a topcoat is needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is e-coating?

A dip paint process where the part is coated in a bath by electric current (cathodic).

Why is weld slag a problem?

MAG silicate slag is non-conductive; e-coat does not cover it and rust starts.

What happens at lap joints?

Gaps trap fluid that seeps out in the oven, causing stains and blisters.

Is e-coat enough outdoors?

Visible outdoor parts usually get a powder topcoat.
