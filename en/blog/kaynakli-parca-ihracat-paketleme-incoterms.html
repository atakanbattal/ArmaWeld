Packing and Incoterms for Exported Welded Parts: EXW, FCA, DAP and Damage-Free Delivery

2026-07-14 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A welded part can leave the workshop in perfect condition and reach the customer rusted, dented or with missing documents. Much of that risk depends on two decisions: choosing the right delivery term (Incoterms) and packing the part for the journey.

Incoterms 2020: a Short Reminder

Incoterms define where cost and risk pass from seller to buyer. They do not govern ownership or payment terms. Three terms are used most for exported welded parts:

TermWhere risk passesExport clearanceMain carriage
EXW (Ex Works)At seller’s premises, before loadingBuyerBuyer
FCA (Free Carrier)On handover to buyer’s carrierSellerBuyer
DAP (Delivered at Place)At destination, before unloadingSellerSeller

Why EXW Is Often the Wrong Choice

Under EXW even loading is the buyer’s responsibility, yet in practice the seller almost always loads the truck. If damage happens during loading, liability becomes unclear. Export clearance is also the buyer’s; a foreign buyer handling a Turkish export declaration is awkward, and the seller may struggle to obtain proof of export for VAT. Where delivery at the works is wanted, FCA is usually cleaner: the seller loads and clears for export, and risk passes on handover to the carrier.

DAP by Truck to the EU

Welded parts from Türkiye to Europe mostly travel by part or full truckload. Under DAP the seller arranges carriage and bears risk up to the destination; import clearance and duties stay with the buyer. For the buyer, a single delivered price is convenient; for the seller, cargo insurance and correct packing become even more important.

Packing: Designed Around the Part

Pallets and crates

  • Wooden pallets and crates must be heat-treated (HT) and marked to ISPM 15.
  • Pallet size is chosen by part weight and mode of transport; heavy parts need special skid pallets.
  • Parts are fixed to the pallet with bolts, straps or blocking; a part that moves damages itself and the vehicle.

Corrosion protection

  • Unpainted black steel rusts easily in transit, especially by sea and in winter.
  • VCI (volatile corrosion inhibitor) film or paper with desiccant bags is an effective solution.
  • Machined surfaces, holes and threads are protected with preservative oil, plugs and caps.
  • Stainless parts must not touch carbon steel; iron contamination leads to rust staining.

Mechanical protection

  • Painted surfaces are separated with edge protectors, foam or cardboard.
  • Protectors are used where straps touch part edges.
  • Long, slender parts are supported at several points so they do not sag.

Lifting and Marking

  • Lifting points are defined and marked on heavy parts; lifting lugs are added to the design where needed.
  • Centre of gravity and gross weight are shown on the crate or pallet.
  • Labels show order number, part number, revision, quantity and package number (e.g. 3/8).
  • Handling symbols such as “this way up”, “keep dry” and “do not stack” follow ISO 780.

Load Securing

For road transport, load securing is calculated to EN 12195-1. For heavy single-piece loads, plan lashing points, number of straps and anti-slip mats that increase friction. A poorly secured load can damage even a well-packed part at the first hard braking.

Documents

  • Commercial invoice and packing list (contents per package, net and gross weight, dimensions),
  • Origin or movement certificate (A.TR, EUR.1 or origin declaration on the invoice, depending on the product),
  • CMR consignment note,
  • Quality documents: material certificates, dimensional and NDT reports, declaration of conformity where required.

Questions to Ask at Quotation

  • What are the delivery term and place?
  • Will parts ship painted or bare? How long must corrosion protection last?
  • Does the customer have a packing specification?
  • Is there a forklift or crane at the unloading point?

Frequently Asked Questions

EXW or FCA?

For delivery at the works, FCA is usually better: the seller loads and clears for export, and risk passes on handover to the carrier. Under EXW, loading and export clearance are the buyer’s.

What is ISPM 15?

The international standard requiring wood packaging to be heat-treated or fumigated against pests and marked accordingly.

How do you ship bare steel without rust?

With VCI film or paper, desiccant bags, preservative oil on machined surfaces and closed packing. Protection should increase with transit time and route.

Who pays import duty under DAP?

The buyer. Under DAP the seller handles carriage to the destination; import clearance and duties are the buyer’s.
