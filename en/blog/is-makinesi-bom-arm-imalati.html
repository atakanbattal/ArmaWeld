Excavator Boom and Arm Fabrication: Fatigue-Resistant Box Sections, Bosses and Pin Bores

2026-07-23 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

An excavator's boom and arm take varying loads on every dig cycle and are expected to survive millions of cycles. Machine builders, attachment makers and special machine companies often have them welded externally. Unlike an ordinary box section, they call for fatigue awareness, pin bore precision and know-how in high-strength steels at the same time.

Structure

  • Box section body: side, top and bottom plates in high-strength steel; internal diaphragms.
  • Bosses and pin bores: cast, forged or thick-plate hubs; cylinder lugs.
  • Reinforcements: extra plates at cylinder connections and knuckles.

Critical Welding Points

  • Boss welds: usually full penetration; the load path runs straight through them.
  • Heat input: in thermomechanically rolled or quenched and tempered steels keep heat input and cooling time (t8/5) within limits (see S700MC and t8/5).
  • Preheat: set by carbon equivalent and thickness for thick bosses and plates.
  • Fatigue details: weld ends, transitions and stiffener ends are critical; toe grinding or TIG dressing is applied where the drawing requires (see fatigue and FAT classes).

Post-Weld Machining

Welding heat distorts pin bores, so they are usually line bored after welding. Bosses are therefore drawn with machining allowance; machining datums and centre distances must be clearly defined.

Inspection

Boss welds and full penetration joints are usually ultrasonically tested, fillets and weld ends checked with MT. Delivery includes pin centre dimensions, material certificates and NDT reports.

Repair or New Build?

Cracked booms can be repaired, but recurring cracks usually point to a design or weld detail problem. For new spare booms or arms without original drawings, reverse engineering is needed (see spare parts for older machines).

Frequently Asked Questions

Which weld is most critical?

Boss (pin bore) welds; usually full penetration and UT inspected.

When are pin bores machined?

Usually after welding, line bored to remove distortion.

What matters with high-strength steel?

Heat input, t8/5 cooling time and preheat where needed.

What if cracks recur?

Review design and weld details; a new build may be the better option.
