Sourcing Welded Parts for Norway: Documents, Standards and Logistics

2026-02-09 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Norway is a strong buyer market in maritime, fisheries and aquaculture equipment, energy, cranes and lifting gear, and steel construction. With labour costs among the highest in Europe, sourcing weld-intensive parts externally is attractive. Norway is not an EU member, however, and that creates some differences in documents and customs.

Not EU, but Inside the EEA

Norway is a member of the European Economic Area (EEA). CE marking, the Machinery Regulation and product rules such as EN 1090 therefore apply in Norway too, and technical expectations are largely the same as in the EU. The difference is customs: the Turkey-EU customs union does not cover Norway. Under the free trade agreement between Turkey and the EFTA states, preferential origin is usually proven with an EUR.1 movement certificate or an origin declaration on the invoice; the A.TR is not valid in Norway (see A.TR, EUR.1 and origin). Whether the product meets the rules of origin should be checked before export. Norway charges import VAT (MVA).

Technical Expectations

  • EN 1090 and EN ISO 3834: the core references for load-bearing structures and machine building (see EN ISO 3834-2).
  • NORSOK: in oil, gas and offshore projects, NORSOK standards (for example M-101 for structural steel fabrication) may be written into the contract. That brings additional procedure qualification, testing and documentation, so clarify it at the start of the project.
  • Corrosion protection: because of the marine and coastal environment, high ISO 12944 corrosivity categories (C5, CX) and long-life paint systems are often required (see ISO 12944 categories).
  • Low-temperature toughness: for structures working in cold climates, steel grades with impact testing at low temperature (e.g. J2, K2, NL) may be specified (see S355 sub-grades).

Language and Business Culture

Technical communication in Norway is largely in English; drawings and documents may be in English. Business culture is direct, transparent and trust-based: hierarchy is flat and problems are discussed openly. Reporting a delay early and honestly is worth far more than explaining it at the last minute.

Logistics

Welded parts from Turkey to Norway can go by road through Europe or by sea. Because the distance is long, packaging, protection against moisture and corrosion, and load securing are especially important. Customs clearance happens separately after the EU border, and that time must be added to the delivery plan. The delivery term and customs responsibility should be stated clearly in the contract (see packaging and Incoterms).

What to Ask the Supplier For

  • Welding quality system documents, welder and procedure qualifications.
  • Material certificates and traceability.
  • Paint system and dry film thickness reports.
  • NDT reports and, where required, a manufacturing record (see data book / MDR).
  • Origin document and export paperwork.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the A.TR used for welded parts shipped to Norway?

No. Norway is not in the EU customs union; under the Turkey-EFTA agreement preferential origin is usually shown with an EUR.1 or an invoice declaration.

Does CE marking apply in Norway?

Yes. As an EEA member, Norway applies CE marking and the related EU product rules.

Is NORSOK required for every project?

No. It is usually required by contract in oil, gas and offshore projects; general machine building is mostly covered by EN standards.

Which language is used with Norwegian buyers?

Technical communication is largely in English.
