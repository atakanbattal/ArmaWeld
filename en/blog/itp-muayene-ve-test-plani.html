What Is an ITP? The Inspection and Test Plan in Welded Fabrication

2026-01-05 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

When you order a large welded part or project, the supplier is usually asked for an ITP (Inspection and Test Plan). An ITP shows in one table which check is done at which stage, to which standard, by whom, and with which record. A well-prepared ITP prevents surprises at delivery.

Why Is an ITP Needed?

  • It creates written agreement between buyer and manufacturer on the scope of checks.
  • It defines in advance which stages the buyer or a third-party inspector will attend.
  • It defines which records go into the final documentation (data book).
  • It catches errors before they move to the next production step.

Types of Inspection Points

CodeMeaningIn practice
H (Hold)Hold pointProduction cannot proceed until the buyer or representative has checked and released
W (Witness)Witness pointBuyer is notified in advance; if they do not attend, production continues
R (Review)Document reviewRecord or document is reviewed by the buyer
M (Monitor)MonitoringBuyer may observe the activity at random, without scheduling
P (Perform)PerformManufacturer performs and records the check

Every hold point stops production. Keep holds to truly critical steps and state the notice period (for example 48 hours) in the ITP.

Columns an ITP Should Have

  • Step number and activity description
  • Reference document (standard, drawing, procedure)
  • Acceptance criterion
  • Extent of inspection (percentage, quantity, every part)
  • Record or report name
  • Inspection code for manufacturer, buyer and any third party (H, W, R, M, P)
  • Signature and date fields

Common Mistakes

  • Making every step a hold: it locks production and extends lead time.
  • No acceptance criterion: "to be checked" does not say against what.
  • No revision control: when the drawing changes, the ITP must be revised too.
  • Forgetting subcontracted steps: galvanizing, painting or machining belong in the ITP as well.

ITP vs Quality Plan

A quality plan (ISO 10005) covers overall quality management of the project: organisation, responsibilities, document control, nonconformity handling. The ITP lists the inspection and test steps within it. On small projects an ITP alone may be enough; large projects usually ask for both.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who prepares the ITP?

Usually the manufacturer prepares it and the buyer approves it. On some projects the buyer provides its own ITP template, which the manufacturer completes based on its processes.

What is the difference between a hold point and a witness point?

At a hold point, production stops until the buyer releases it. At a witness point, the buyer is notified; if they do not attend, production continues.

Is the ITP part of the contract?

An approved ITP is usually treated as a contract annex, so changes should be managed with revision numbers.

Does a small order need an ITP?

Simple parts often do not; a control plan or first article approval is enough. ITPs are common for EN 1090 EXC3, pressure equipment and critical machine parts.
