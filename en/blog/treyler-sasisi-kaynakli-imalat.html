Trailer and Semi-Trailer Chassis Fabrication: Critical Welding Points

2026-09-21 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A trailer chassis works for years under variable load, vibration, impact and corrosion. That is why the real danger in chassis welding is not static failure but fatigue cracking. Konya is one of Turkey’s main centres for trailer and semi-trailer production; this article collects the points that matter for welding in chassis fabrication.

Main Chassis Components

  • Main beams: usually welded I-beams made from plate; web and flange plates are joined with long seams.
  • Cross members: transverse members connecting the main beams.
  • Kingpin area: the highly loaded plate and reinforcements connecting to the tractor.
  • Axle and suspension mountings: hanger brackets, load spreading plates.
  • Landing gear mountings, side guards, rear bumper.

Material Choice

S355 structural steels are common in conventional chassis. To save weight, thermomechanically rolled high-strength steels such as S500MC and S700MC are also used for beams and some components. With high-strength steels:

  • Filler metal strength and heat input range should follow the steelmaker’s recommendations,
  • Excessive heat input can cause strength loss in the heat-affected zone,
  • The fatigue strength of weld toes does not rise in proportion to the base metal’s strength.

For cold operating conditions, impact toughness (e.g. J2 or K2) should be stated on the drawing (see S355 sub-grades).

Welding the Main Beams

The long longitudinal seams between web and flange account for most of the weld length in a chassis. A smooth, continuous weld here matters for both strength and distortion. On long straight seams submerged arc welding gives a high deposition rate and consistent penetration along the seam; MAG welding is used in short or hard-to-reach areas.

Points to watch:

  • Fillet weld size at web-to-flange joints per drawing, with partial or full penetration if required,
  • Butt splices in flange plates made with full penetration and volumetrically tested,
  • Flange splices placed away from high-stress zones at the design stage.

Camber

Beams that should stay straight under load are given a slight upward pre-curve during fabrication. This is achieved by cutting the web with camber or by controlled weld sequencing. Camber value and tolerance should be defined on the drawing and measured and recorded after fabrication.

Distortion Control

  • Symmetrical weld sequence (balanced welding from both sides),
  • Suitable fixtures and tacking,
  • Pre-setting flange plates against angular distortion,
  • Controlled flame straightening where needed (see flame straightening).

Fatigue-Sensitive Details

Most chassis damage starts at certain details:

Kingpin and Axle Areas

Kingpins and coupling devices are approved components; welding, installation and reinforcement details should follow the component maker’s instructions and the vehicle manufacturer’s design. These areas usually get the strictest inspection. Vehicle type approval and regulatory compliance are the vehicle manufacturer’s responsibility; the welding shop provides fabrication to drawing and instructions, with records.

Inspection

  • Visual inspection and dimensional checks of all welds,
  • Magnetic particle testing (MT) of critical fillet welds,
  • Ultrasonic testing (UT) of flange butt welds,
  • Camber, flatness and diagonal measurements.

Surface Protection

A chassis is exposed to road salt and moisture. Blasting and painting or hot-dip galvanising are common options. If galvanising is chosen, vent and drain holes must be planned at the design stage (see galvanising welded parts).

Frequently Asked Questions

What damage is most common in trailer chassis?

Fatigue cracks, especially at cross member connection ends, brackets welded to flanges and weld start-stop points.

Why are main beams welded with submerged arc?

On long straight seams SAW gives a high deposition rate and consistent penetration along the seam.

What is camber?

An upward pre-curve given to the beam during fabrication so that it stays straight under load.

Does high-strength steel make the chassis lighter?

Yes, but welding parameters must be chosen carefully, and weld toe fatigue strength does not rise as much as the base metal’s strength.
