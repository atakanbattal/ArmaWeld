Box Girder Fabrication: Welding Sequence, Diaphragms and the Closing Plate

2026-07-13 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A box girder is a closed section of two webs and two flanges. It resists torsion far better than an I-beam of the same weight, which is why it is used for overhead crane main girders, bridge girders, machinery booms and large machine bodies. Being closed raises its own fabrication question: once the last plate is on, the inside is out of reach.

Sequence: Finish Before Closing

A typical sequence: diaphragms and longitudinal stiffeners are placed on the bottom flange, both webs are fitted, all internal diaphragm welds are made and inspected, then the top flange closes the box. Inspection of internal welds and any internal coating must be completed before closing.

The Closing Weld

The last flange can only be welded from outside. If full penetration is required, it is usually a single-sided butt weld on a permanent backing bar; on fatigue-loaded girders the detail category must be considered in design (see fatigue and FAT classes). The long, straight corner seams suit submerged arc welding (see submerged arc welding).

Twist, Camber and Distortion

  • If the four longitudinal seams are not welded symmetrically, the girder twists and sweeps.
  • Crane girder camber is produced by the cutting profile of the webs.
  • Diaphragm spacing and cut-outs are designed to prevent web buckling.

Interior: Sealed or Vented?

Fully seal-welded boxes have low internal corrosion risk, but sealing should be tested. Boxes to be galvanised must have vent and drain holes, otherwise dangerous pressure builds up in the bath (see welded parts for galvanising). The chosen approach must be stated clearly on the drawing.

Inspection and Documents

Web and flange splices are usually ultrasonically tested, fillet seams visually and with MT where required (see UT or RT). Delivery includes a dimensional report (camber, twist, sweep), material certificates, WPS/WPQR and NDT reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why use a box girder?

It resists torsion far better than an I-beam of the same weight.

How is the closing plate welded?

From outside only; for full penetration usually single-sided on a backing bar.

When are internal welds inspected?

Before the closing plate goes on.

What does a box for galvanising need?

Vent and drain holes; otherwise dangerous pressure builds in the bath.
