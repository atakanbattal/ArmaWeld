Blast Cleaning and Sa 2½: Ordering Surface Preparation for Welded Parts Correctly

2026-08-28 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

What decides the life of a paint system most is not the paint but the surface underneath. On welded parts, mill scale, rust, slag and spatter ruin adhesion. That is why specifications so often say "blast to Sa 2½". Is that phrase enough on its own?

ISO 8501-1 Cleanliness Grades

  • Sa 1: light blast cleaning; loose mill scale and rust removed.
  • Sa 2: thorough blast cleaning; most mill scale and rust removed.
  • Sa 2½: very thorough; no visible mill scale, rust or paint, only slight shadows or stains. The most common request for industrial coatings.
  • Sa 3: visually clean steel; for special cases such as thermal spray.

These grades describe the surface, not the welds and edges. For those, specify ISO 8501-3 preparation grades (P1, P2, P3) as well (see ISO 8501-3).

Surface Profile

Adhesion needs a certain roughness. ISO 8503 classes profiles as fine, medium and coarse; the paint maker's data sheet says which is required. High-build systems usually need medium. Writing only "Sa 2½" leaves the profile undefined.

Dust, Salts and Time to Primer

  • Dust left after blasting reduces adhesion; it can be checked to ISO 8502-3.
  • Critical applications such as marine may require soluble salt measurement.
  • Blasted steel rusts quickly in humidity; prime soon after blasting.

Points Specific to Welded Parts

  • Thin sheet: aggressive blasting can ripple thin panels; choose abrasive and pressure accordingly.
  • Masking: machined faces, threads and bearings must be protected before blasting.
  • Closed sections: abrasive that gets inside must be removed; grit trapped behind vent holes causes trouble.
  • Galvanising: parts to be galvanised get chemical preparation instead of blasting (see welded parts for galvanising).

What to Write in the Order

Cleanliness grade (e.g. Sa 2½), ISO 8501-3 preparation grade, surface profile, coating system and corrosivity category (see ISO 12944), surfaces to mask, and inspection records.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Sa 2½ mean?

Very thorough blast cleaning under ISO 8501-1; no visible mill scale, rust or old paint.

Is "Sa 2½" enough in an order?

No; also specify surface profile and the ISO 8501-3 weld preparation grade.

How long can blasted steel wait?

Not long; it rusts quickly in humidity, so prime soon.

Are galvanised parts blasted?

Usually not; galvanising uses chemical preparation.
