Fatigue in Welded Joints: FAT Classes, S-N Curves and Details That Extend Life

2026-03-02 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A welded part that passes a static check comfortably can still crack within a few years under fluctuating loads. Trailer chassis, crane booms, vibrating machine frames and agricultural equipment are typical examples. This article explains how fatigue in welded joints is assessed and which design and fabrication decisions extend service life.

Why Welds Are the Weak Point in Fatigue

  • Geometric stress concentration: the weld toe and root are points where the section changes abruptly.
  • Micro-defects: the toe always has small undercuts, slag traces or sharp transitions, so cracks need little time to initiate.
  • Residual stresses: welding leaves tensile residual stresses near yield. For welded joints the stress range (Δσ) therefore matters more than mean stress.

An important consequence: for as-welded joints, using higher-strength steel does not significantly increase fatigue strength. In fatigue, detail geometry matters more than material grade.

FAT Classes and the S-N Curve

IIW (International Institute of Welding) recommendations and EN 1993-1-9 classify welded details by FAT class or detail category. The FAT value is the stress range (MPa) the detail withstands at 2 million cycles. For welded details the S-N curve slope is typically m = 3:

N = 2 × 10⁶ × (FAT / Δσ)³

The slope is powerful: doubling the stress range cuts life to one eighth; reducing it by 20% roughly doubles life.

Typical Detail Classes (Guidance)

DetailApprox. FAT (MPa)
Unwelded plate with machined edges≈ 160

Transverse butt weld as-welded≈ 80-90

Load-carrying fillet weld, root failure (on throat)≈ 36

Values vary with thickness, geometry and standard; real designs must use the detail tables of the relevant code. The table still shows a key fact: under the same load, a detail failing from a fillet weld root is more than three times weaker in stress range than a ground butt weld.

Design Decisions That Extend Life

  • Move welds away from high-stress zones: place attachments, lugs and stiffeners near the neutral axis rather than where bending moment peaks.
  • Prevent root failure: in load-carrying joints, consider full-penetration welds instead of fillets.
  • Smooth thickness transitions: use tapers such as 1:4 between plates of different thickness.
  • Soften attachment ends: chamfered or radiused ends on long longitudinal attachments reduce stress concentration.
  • Avoid intermittent welds in fatigue zones: every start and stop is a potential crack initiation site.

Fabrication Methods That Extend Life

Post-weld improvement methods smooth the toe geometry or turn residual stress into compression:

  • Burr grinding: a small-radius groove is ground at the toe, smoothing the transition and removing micro-defects.
  • TIG dressing: the toe is remelted without filler to give a smoother profile.
  • High-frequency mechanical impact (HFMI): impact treatment at the toe introduces compressive residual stress.

IIW recommendations credit these methods with additional strength under defined conditions. They do not help against root failure, and their application must be documented.

ISO 5817 Annex C: Fatigue-Loaded Welds

The 2014 edition of ISO 5817 defines quality levels such as B125, B90 and C63 with additional requirements for fatigue-loaded structures. They set tighter limits on toe angle, undercut and misalignment, which directly affect fatigue. On a fatigue-critical part, the drawing should specify the appropriate level rather than just "B".

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a FAT class?

The stress range (MPa) a welded detail withstands at 2 million load cycles. IIW recommendations and EN 1993-1-9 classify details by this value.

Does higher-strength steel increase fatigue life?

For as-welded joints, not significantly. Detail geometry and weld toe quality govern fatigue strength.

How does stress range relate to life?

For welded details the S-N slope is typically 3; doubling the stress range cuts life to one eighth.

What does toe grinding do?

It removes the sharp transition and micro-defects at the weld toe, delaying fatigue crack initiation. It has no effect on root failure.
