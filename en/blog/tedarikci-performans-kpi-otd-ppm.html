Welding Supplier Performance KPIs: OTD, PPM and the Supplier Scorecard

2025-12-29 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The answer to "is this supplier good?" should rest on numbers, not feelings. A supplier scorecard lets purchasing and quality teams discuss the same data. But an indicator only works if its definition is clear; otherwise the supplier reports 98% on-time delivery while the customer sees 80%.

Key Indicators

OTD (on-time delivery)Order lines delivered on time / total order linesWhich date counts: first confirmed or revised?

Defining OTD Correctly

Most OTD disputes come from definitions. Points to clarify:

  • Reference date: the first date confirmed by the supplier, or a date agreed later? Measuring against constantly revised dates makes performance look better than it is.
  • Tolerance window: for example, 2 days early to 0 days late counts as "on time". Very early deliveries can also cause warehouse problems.
  • Measurement point: leaving the supplier, or arriving at the customer? The difference is large between EXW and DAP.
  • Unit: order line or quantity? If 99 of 100 parts on a line arrive, the line is late.

How Meaningful Is PPM for Welded Parts?

PPM is a strong indicator for small parts in high volumes. But if a few hundred large frames are bought per year, a single rejection puts PPM in the thousands and makes the indicator volatile. For this kind of work, the number of NCRs, repair hours and defect type (dimensional, weld, surface, documentation) are more useful. Sorting by defect type also makes root causes easier to find (see NCR and 8D).

Example Calculation

In one quarter 40 order lines were opened, 36 were delivered within the agreed date window and 2 arrived short. OTD = 36/40 = 90%. Adding the in-full condition gives OTIF = 34/40 = 85%. If 1,200 parts were delivered in the same period and 3 were rejected, PPM = 3/1,200 × 1,000,000 = 2,500.

Building a Supplier Scorecard

  • Choose few but correct indicators; a fifteen-indicator scorecard is read by nobody.
  • Share each definition with the supplier and compare it with their data.
  • Make the weighting explicit (e.g. delivery 40%, quality 40%, communication and documentation 20%).
  • Show the trend; a three-to-six-month trend says more than one bad month.
  • Discuss the scorecard in a regular meeting; just e-mailing it changes nothing.

What Indicators Do Not Show

A good scorecard does not guarantee that the supplier always makes the right part. Supplier audits, process reviews and in-process inspections for critical parts complement the indicators (see supplier audits). Customer-caused delays, such as late drawing revisions or pending approvals, should also be recorded separately; otherwise the supplier is marked down unfairly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is OTD?

The share of order lines delivered on time out of all order lines; the reference date and tolerance window must be clearly defined.

What is the difference between OTD and OTIF?

OTIF also requires delivery in full; short deliveries show up in OTIF.

How is PPM calculated?

Rejected parts divided by delivered parts, multiplied by 1,000,000.

Which indicator suits large, low-volume parts?

Number of NCRs, repair hours and defect-type breakdown are more meaningful than PPM.
