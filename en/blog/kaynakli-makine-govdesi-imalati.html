Welded Machine Bases and Frames: Moving from Castings to Fabrications

2026-10-01 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Machine tools, presses, pumps, compressors and special machines all stand on a heavy, stiff and dimensionally stable base or frame. These have traditionally been castings. But for low and medium volumes, large sizes and frequently changing designs, a welded steel fabrication is a serious alternative.

Casting or Weldment?

CriterionCasting (grey / ductile iron)Welded steel
Pattern / tooling costYes, expensive for low volumesNone
Design changesPattern must changeFast, by drawing revision
Stiffness / weightLower elastic modulus, thicker sections neededHigher modulus of steel, same stiffness with a lighter frame
Vibration dampingHigh in grey ironLower; can be improved with rib layout and filling
Size limitDepends on foundry capacityVery large frames possible by splitting into sections
Lead time (first part)Pattern and casting timeUsually shorter

For high-volume, small, damping-critical bases, casting still has the edge. For one-offs or small series, large frames and frequently revised machines, a weldment is usually more economical.

Designing for Stiffness

  • Closed box sections: far higher torsional stiffness than open sections.
  • Rib layout: diagonal or honeycomb ribs give high stiffness even with thin plates; rib welds must be easy to reach.
  • Load path: loads from bearings and mounting points should be carried to the base through continuous plates.
  • Plate thickness: under machined faces, choose enough thickness for machining allowance and deflection.

Vibration

The frame’s natural frequency should be well away from the machine’s operating frequencies. Increasing stiffness raises it. Where damping is needed, the designer may consider filling cavities with special materials or changing the rib layout.

Dimensional Stability and Stress Relief

Welding leaves residual stresses. As material is removed in machining these stresses redistribute and the part can change shape after machining. So stress relief before machining is common for precision frames:

  • Thermal stress relief: controlled heating in a furnace and slow cooling; the most effective method.
  • Vibratory stress relief: used as an alternative in some applications for frames too big for a furnace.

Which method is required should be stated on the drawing, based on the precision needed and the designer’s requirement.

Machining Allowance and Datums

  • Leave enough machining allowance on machined faces, taking distortion into account.
  • Define datum faces or points for post-weld machining in advance.
  • Plan welds under machined faces so machining does not expose porosity or lack of fusion.

See our separate article on planning machining after welding.

Lifting, Transport and Installation

  • Lifting points (lugs, holes) sized for the load and shown on the drawing,
  • Levelling pads or seats for levelling screws,
  • Openings for cables, hydraulics and lubrication,
  • For very large frames, splitting to transport size with bolted joints (see welded or bolted).

Typical Fabrication Sequence

  1. Cutting and bending,
  2. Fit-up and tacking in a fixture,
  3. Welding with a balanced sequence,
  4. Visual inspection and required NDT,
  5. Stress relief (if required),
  6. Blasting and primer,
  7. Machining,
  8. Dimensional check and topcoat.

What the Drawing Should Contain

  • General welding tolerance (ISO 13920 class) and functional dimensions (see ISO 13920),
  • Flatness and surface roughness of machined faces,
  • Stress relief requirement,
  • Weld sizes and quality level,
  • Paint system and protection of machined faces.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a welded base cheaper than a casting?

For one-offs and small series usually yes, as there is no pattern cost. For high-volume small bases casting may be more economical.

Does a welded frame damp vibration as well as a casting?

Not as well as grey iron, but vibration behaviour can be improved through stiffness, rib layout and filling.

Why is stress relief needed?

Residual welding stresses can make the part change shape after machining; stress relief improves dimensional stability.

How are very large frames transported?

They are split into sections to transport size and designed for bolting together on site.
