What is WPQR? Welding Procedure Qualification Records

2026-05-02 9 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A WPQR (Welding Procedure Qualification Record) is a document certified by test results that proves a welding procedure delivers the required mechanical properties. A WPS (Welding Procedure Specification) only defines how to weld; the WPQR proves that the procedure actually achieves the desired properties.

Fundamental rule: Companies manufacturing under EN ISO 3834-2 and EN 1090-2 must link every WPS to a valid WPQR. Welding with an unqualified procedure is not acceptable.

EN ISO 15614-1: Qualification Process for Steel

Welding procedure qualification for steel is performed under EN ISO 15614-1. The process involves:

  1. Prepare pWPS (preliminary WPS): Draft welding parameters.
  2. Weld the test piece: Using production equipment and personnel under workshop conditions.
  3. Non-destructive examination (NDE): Visual (VT), penetrant (PT), ultrasonic (UT) or radiographic (RT).
  4. Destructive tests: Tensile, bend, hardness survey, Charpy impact, macro examination.
  5. Write and approve WPQR: All results recorded; if acceptable, WPS is formally qualified.

Range of Approval

A WPS qualified by a WPQR covers a limited range of application. Any weld outside that range needs a new qualification:

  • Material group: by ISO/TR 15608 group number (1.1, 1.2, 2, 3, 8.1 etc.). A test in one group also covers certain other groups according to the table in the standard.
  • Thickness: for a test piece of 3–12 mm, roughly 0.5t (minimum 3 mm) to 2t; for 12–100 mm, 0.5t to 2t. For single-run or high heat input welds the upper limit is narrower.
  • Diameter (pipe): a range starting at half the test diameter is qualified; for small diameters both the lower and upper limits are narrower.
  • Position: without impact or hardness requirements, a test in one position covers all others except vertical down (PG); with such requirements the positions with the highest and lowest heat input must be tested.
  • Consumables, gas and heat input: where impact requirements apply, heat input may be at most 25% higher than in the test; where hardness requirements apply, at most 25% lower.

Important: EN ISO 15614-1 covers steels and nickel alloys. Welding procedures for aluminium and its alloys are qualified to EN ISO 15614-2. Always check the current tables of the standard for exact ranges.

Routes to Qualify a WPS: EN ISO 15607

Not every welding procedure needs a laboratory test. EN ISO 15607 defines five ways to qualify a WPS:

StandardRouteWhen is it used?
EN ISO 15614Welding procedure testThe most comprehensive route. Usually mandatory for EXC3, EXC4 and pressure equipment.
EN ISO 15613Pre-production welding testWhen a standard test piece cannot represent the real part, a part-like sample is welded.
EN ISO 15612Standard welding procedureUse of a published standard procedure; for low-risk work and limited materials.
EN ISO 15611Previous welding experienceThe manufacturer documents successful past production with the same procedure.
EN ISO 15610Tested welding consumablesBased on consumable approval; the most limited route.

Under EN 1090-2, routes 15610, 15611 and 15612 can be accepted in EXC2 for certain materials and conditions. In EXC3 and EXC4, procedures must be qualified by an EN ISO 15614-1 test or an EN ISO 15613 pre-production test. So on a project with a high execution class it is not enough for a supplier to say "we have WPQRs"; ask which route they were qualified by.

EN ISO 15614-1:2017 Level 1 and Level 2

The 2017 edition of the standard introduced two test levels:

  • Level 1: closer to the ASME IX approach; fewer tests and a wider range of approval.
  • Level 2: the traditional European approach; with impact and hardness tests it gives a narrower but more reliable range of approval.

A procedure qualified to level 2 also covers level 1, but not the other way round. Work under EN 1090-2 is based on level 2. When you review a WPQR, the level it was issued to is one of the first things to check.

Validity of a WPQR

A WPQR qualified under EN ISO 15614 is indefinitely valid — no renewal required. However, re-qualification is needed if:

  • The welding process changes (e.g. MAG to TIG)
  • The material group changes
  • Thickness exceeds the qualified range
  • Consumables change significantly

Checklist for Reviewing a Supplier's WPQRs

When your purchasing or quality team reviews a supplier's WPQR list, ask these questions:

  1. Process: is the process for your part (for example 135 or 121) on the list?
  2. Material group: is your steel (for example S355 group 1.2, S690QL group 3) within the qualified groups?
  3. Thickness and diameter: are your thinnest and thickest parts within the qualified range?
  4. Joint type: a butt weld qualification covers fillet welds, but not the other way round. If you have full-penetration butt welds, a butt weld qualification is required.
  5. Impact test temperature: if the project requires impact values at -20 °C or -40 °C, the WPQR must have been tested at that temperature.
  6. Heat treatment: if the project requires PWHT, the WPQR must have been made on a heat-treated test piece.
  7. Examining body and level: who signed the document, and to which standard and level was it issued?

How Long Does a WPQR Take?

Welding the test piece and non-destructive testing are usually finished within a few days. Destructive tests can take one to three weeks depending on the laboratory's workload. For a material or thickness without a qualification, this time has to be planned at quotation stage. With a supplier whose existing WPQRs already cover your job, the waiting time disappears completely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a WPS and a WPQR?

A WPS is the instruction that tells the welder how to weld. A WPQR is the record of the inspection and test results of a test piece welded to that instruction, proving that the procedure works.

Does a WPQR expire?

A WPQR qualified to EN ISO 15614-1 does not expire. But if an essential variable (process, material group, thickness range, heat treatment and so on) changes, a new qualification is needed.

Who approves a WPQR?

An independent examining body (for example TÜV, Bureau Veritas, DNV) that witnesses the tests and evaluates the results. For pressure equipment this body must be a notified body or a recognised third-party organisation.

Can a WPQR be used by another company?

No. A WPQR is tied to the manufacturer that carried out the test and to its technical control. Another company's WPQR cannot be taken over.
