What Is First Article Inspection (FAI)? What to Check on Welded Parts Before Series Production

2026-11-16 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

When you have a new welded part made by a supplier for the first time, the biggest risk is that problems only appear when the first shipment reaches your assembly line. First article inspection (FAI) means measuring and documenting the first part produced against every requirement of the drawing, and having it approved by the customer, before series production starts. Known as PPAP in automotive and AS9102 in aerospace, this approach is increasingly standard in machinery and steel fabrication too.

When Is a First Article Inspection Required?

  • When a new part is produced for the first time
  • When the drawing revision changes (dimensions, material, weld details)
  • When the production site, fixture, welding process or sub-supplier changes
  • When the part has not been produced for a long time (in practice 12 months or more)
  • When a recurring nonconformity occurred in previous batches

What Should an FAI Report for a Welded Part Contain?

SectionContent
Ballooned drawingA copy of the drawing with every dimension and requirement numbered
Dimensional reportNominal value, tolerance, measured value and result for each number. ISO 13920 class for general tolerances
Weld inspectionVisual testing (ISO 17637), weld sizes, assessment against the EN ISO 5817 quality level
NDT reportsMT, PT, UT or RT results required by the drawing or specification
Material documentsEN 10204 3.1 certificates and the match between part and heat number
Welding recordsWPS numbers used and welder IDs
Surface treatmentCoating thickness measurements if painted or galvanized
PhotosPhotos of critical details and the overall part

Points Specific to Welded Parts

Made with the series production method

The first article must be made with the fixture, welding sequence and WPS that will be used in series production. If a carefully hand-made sample is approved and the series is then made a different way, the FAI loses its meaning.

Measured after welding and straightening

Welding distortion changes dimensions. The dimensional report should be prepared after welding, straightening and any heat treatment are complete. If surfaces are machined after welding, they are measured after machining as well.

Critical dimensions defined in advance

Dimensions critical to function, such as mounting holes, mating faces and bearing seats, should be marked on the drawing and always measured in the FAI. They are then followed by sampling in series production.

Tolerance class stated on the drawing

If the drawing has no general tolerance note such as "ISO 13920-BF", the supplier and the customer may judge the same part by different criteria. This missing note is the most common cause of FAI disputes.

On-Site or Remote Approval?

For critical parts, having a customer representative witness the FAI in the workshop is the safest route. For customers abroad, remote approval with a detailed report, photos and, where needed, a live video call is common practice. Adding the calibration records of the measuring equipment to the report increases confidence in a remote approval.

How to Put FAI in Your Order

  • Scope of the FAI: how many parts, which dimensions, which tests
  • Approval method: on-site witness, report or video
  • That series production starts after FAI approval
  • Whether the FAI part is shipped and how it is marked
  • Time allowed for approval; add it to the delivery schedule

Frequently Asked Questions

Is FAI the same as PPAP?

No. FAI is the check that shows the first part conforms to the drawing. PPAP is a broader approval package used in automotive; besides an FAI-like dimensional report it includes documents such as the process flow, FMEA, control plan and process capability.

Does first article inspection extend lead time?

Preparing the report usually adds one to three working days, and the approval time must also be planned. In return, the cost and delay of an error discovered in series production are far greater.

On how many parts is an FAI done?

One part is usually enough. For multi-cavity fixtures or parts made at more than one welding station, measuring one part from each fixture or station is recommended.
