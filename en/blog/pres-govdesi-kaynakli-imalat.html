Welded Press Frame Manufacturing: Heavy Plate, Stiffness and Dimensional Stability

2026-04-16 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A press frame closes the entire force the machine produces within itself. The bigger the force, the thicker the plates and the deeper the welds. Making press frames welded instead of cast saves time and often cost for one-offs or small batches (see fabrication instead of casting). That advantage, however, depends on getting the welding and machining sequence right.

Frame Types

  • C-frame (open gap): gives front access; gap opening (deflection) is the key design criterion.
  • H-frame / closed frame: stiffer, for higher capacities.
  • Plate (laminated) frame: several thick plates joined by connecting pieces.
  • Column presses: welded box upper and lower tables, machined columns.

Heavy Plate Welding

Plates of 50 mm and above are common in press frames. At these thicknesses:

  • Preheat and interpass temperature control are needed (see preheat calculation).
  • Hydrogen-controlled consumables and dried electrodes/flux are used.
  • Joint geometry (double V, U preparation) matters for both penetration and filler volume (see joint preparation).
  • Lamellar tearing risk is assessed where joints are loaded through thickness.

Inspection

For main load-bearing full-penetration welds, UT suits thick sections better than radiography (see UT or RT). Fillet and T-joints are checked by MT. Inspection scope (what percentage, which welds) should be defined on the drawing or in the ITP.

Stress Relief and Machining

Heavy plate welding leaves high residual stress. When the frame is machined, those stresses are released and machined surfaces can move over time. For precision press frames the sequence is usually: welding → stress relief → rough machining → (if needed) second heat treatment → finish machining (see stress relief and post-weld machining). Frame size determines the required furnace and large machine tool capacity; both should be verified at quotation stage.

Questions for the Supplier

  • Is there a qualified WPS and welder approval for heavy plate?
  • How is preheat applied and is it recorded?
  • What level are the UT personnel?
  • Where and up to what size can stress relief and large machining be done?
  • Crane capacity and the ability to turn the frame.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are press frames welded?

For one-offs and small batches they can be produced faster and often cheaper than castings.

Does a press frame need stress relief?

Usually yes for thick welded frames that will be precision machined; otherwise machined surfaces can move.

Which NDT is used on thick press plates?

UT on full-penetration welds, MT on fillet and T-joints.

What is most critical on a C-frame press?

Gap opening (deflection); stiffness is the main design criterion.
