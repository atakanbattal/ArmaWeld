Types of Welding Wire: G3Si1 (SG2), G4Si1 (SG3), Flux-Cored and More

2026-05-13 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The welding wire determines the strength, toughness and chemistry of the weld metal. The wrong wire gives a weld that does not meet the specification, even with the right parameters. This article summarises the main wire types used in MIG/MAG, flux-cored and submerged arc welding, and how to read the code on the label.

Carbon Steel MIG/MAG Wires (ISO 14341)

Most structural steel work is done with two wires:

ISO 14341-AOld DIN nameClose AWS A5.18Characteristics
G3Si1SG2ER70S-6 (approx.)General purpose, standard choice for S235–S355
G4Si1SG3ER70S-6 (approx.)Higher Mn and Si; slightly higher strength, more tolerant of light rust or mill scale

Note: AWS and ISO classes are not identical; select according to the system the specification calls for.

How to Read the Classification

Example: ISO 14341-A G 42 4 M21 3Si1

  • G: wire and weld metal for gas-shielded welding.
  • 42: minimum weld metal yield strength about 420 MPa.
  • 4: at least 47 J impact energy at −40 °C.
  • M21: gas group used in the test (argon + 15–25% CO₂, ISO 14175).
  • 3Si1: chemical composition symbol of the wire.

The key point: the mechanical values were obtained with a specific gas. Welded with 100% CO₂, the same wire can give different strength and toughness, which is why the WPS defines gas and wire together (see shielding gas selection).

High-Strength Steel Wires (ISO 16834)

On S460 and higher steels the weld metal must also be strong enough. Wires classified to ISO 16834, shown for example as G 69 or G 89, target yield strengths around 690 and 890 MPa. With these steels heat input and cooling time matter as much as the wire (see welding S700MC). For fillet welds, an undermatching wire may be used if the design allows; that decision belongs to engineering.

Flux-Cored Wires (ISO 17632)

Flux-cored wire is a tube filled with powder. Rutile cored wires run easily in position and give a smooth bead; basic cored wires give better toughness and crack resistance; metal cored wires offer high deposition with little slag. See our flux-cored wire article for details.

Stainless Steel Wires (ISO 14343)

  • 308LSi: for 304/304L.
  • 316LSi: for 316/316L.
  • 309LSi: stainless to carbon steel joints and buffer layers.
  • 2209: for duplex 2205.

The “Si” versions give a more fluid pool and better wetting.

Aluminium Wires (ISO 18273)

The two most common are 4043 (AlSi5) and 5356 (AlMg5). 4043 is more fluid and less prone to hot cracking; 5356 gives higher strength and a better colour match after anodising. The choice depends on the parent alloy and service conditions (see aluminium welding).

Submerged Arc Wires (ISO 14171)

In submerged arc welding, wire and flux are classified as a pair; for example S2 or S2Si wire gives certain mechanical values with a certain flux. Changing the flux can require re-evaluation just as changing the wire does.

Diameter and Packaging

  • 0.8 mm: thin sheet, low current.
  • 1.0 mm: thin and medium thickness, positional welding.
  • 1.2 mm: the most common diameter for medium and thick parts.
  • 1.6 mm: high deposition, heavy welds in the flat position.

Wire typically comes on 15–18 kg spools or, in series production, in drums of 250 kg and more. Drums reduce stops for wire changes.

Storage and Traceability

Keep wire dry and in closed packaging; do not use wire with rusty or contaminated surfaces. Where the specification requires, the wire batch number and EN 10204 certificate are recorded so it is traceable which consumable each weld was made with (see material certificates).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between SG2 and SG3 wire?

SG3 (G4Si1) contains more manganese and silicon; it gives slightly higher strength and tolerates lightly contaminated surfaces better. Both are common for general structural steel.

What does G 42 4 M21 3Si1 mean?

A gas-shielded wire with about 420 MPa minimum yield, 47 J at −40 °C, tested with M21 gas, with 3Si1 chemistry.

What is the most common welding wire diameter?

1.2 mm is the most common for medium and thick structural steel; 0.8–1.0 mm is preferred for thin sheet.

4043 or 5356 for aluminium?

4043 is more fluid and less prone to hot cracking; 5356 gives higher strength. The choice depends on the parent alloy and service.
