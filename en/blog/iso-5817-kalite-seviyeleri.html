ISO 5817 Quality Levels B, C and D: Which One Should You Specify?

2025-11-17 9 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

On a weldment drawing, the note "ISO 5817-C" defines in a single line how much imperfection the welds may contain. It drives price, lead time and inspection scope. Yet many drawings either omit the quality level or specify B "just to be safe". This article explains what ISO 5817 is, the practical differences between levels B, C and D, and how to choose the right one.

What Is ISO 5817?

EN ISO 5817 sets acceptance limits for imperfections in fusion welds (excluding electron and laser beam welding) in steel, nickel, titanium and their alloys. It covers material thicknesses from 0.5 mm. The current edition dates from 2023. Aluminium has its own standard: EN ISO 10042.

The standard uses the imperfection numbers of ISO 6520-1. For example, 100 is a crack, 2017 surface pore, 5011 continuous undercut, 502 excess weld metal and 5213 insufficient throat thickness. Each imperfection has a separate limit for three levels:

LevelMeaningTypical use
BStringentFatigue-loaded structures, EXC3 and EXC4 work, pressure equipment, lifting equipment
CIntermediateGeneral steel structures, machine frames, EXC2 work
DModerateLow-stress secondary parts, railings, covers, guards, EXC1 work

One important point: ISO 5817 is not a fitness-for-purpose standard. A weld meeting level D is not automatically adequate for its function. The designer chooses the level according to load, safety and production economics.

The values are summarised from the standard; some rows have different limits for thin sheet from 0.5 to 3 mm. Always use the current text of the standard for exact values.

At level B, surface porosity and insufficient throat are not permitted at all and undercut is almost zero. That means slower, more controlled welding, more grinding and more frequent repairs.

Additional Fatigue Levels: B 125, B 90, C 63

Annex C of ISO 5817 defines additional requirements for fatigue-loaded structures. Designations such as B 125, B 90 and C 63 correspond to the fatigue class (FAT) used in design. On parts with variable loads, such as trailer chassis, crane booms or vibrating machine frames, the drawing should state the additional level matching the fatigue calculation instead of a plain "B". These levels set tighter limits on toe angle, undercut and root-side imperfections, and sometimes require weld toe grinding.

Link with EN 1090

For steel structures, EN 1090-2 sets the default quality level by execution class:

  • EXC1: quality level D
  • EXC2: quality level C (D allowed for some imperfection types)
  • EXC3: quality level B
  • EXC4: quality level B plus additional requirements

So if your drawing says EXC2, level C applies even without a separate note. Machine parts outside the scope of EN 1090 have no such default, and the level must be written on the drawing.

Link with NDT Acceptance Levels

The ISO 5817 quality level also determines the acceptance levels used in NDT reports. ISO 17635 gives the correlation:

MethodFor BFor CFor D
VT (ISO 17637)ISO 5817 BISO 5817 CISO 5817 D

UT (ISO 11666)Level 2Level 3Level 3
MT / PT (ISO 23278 / 23277)2X2X3X

So when an inspection report says "ISO 11666 AL2", the weld was assessed against level B.

How the Level Affects Cost

Cost rises with the quality level, for several reasons:

  • Welding time: at level B, profile and undercut are controlled more tightly, so travel speed drops.
  • Grinding and repair: a small pore accepted at level C needs repair at level B.
  • Inspection scope: higher levels usually come with a higher NDT rate.
  • Welder and procedure: welder experience and WPS coverage matter more at level B.

In practice, the most economical approach is to mark critical welds separately and leave the rest at C or D, rather than writing B for the whole part. On a machine frame, for instance, motor mounting welds can be B, general frame welds C, and sheet cover and guard welds D.

How to Write It on the Drawing

  • General note: "Weld quality level EN ISO 5817-C unless otherwise stated."
  • For critical welds, in the tail of the weld symbol: "ISO 5817-B".
  • For fatigue-loaded welds, an additional level such as "ISO 5817-B 90".
  • If needed, inspection scope: "VT 100 %, UT 10 % (ISO 17640, ISO 11666 AL3)".
  • For aluminium parts, reference EN ISO 10042 instead of ISO 5817.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ISO 5817 B better than C?

B is more stringent and allows fewer imperfections. But "better" is not always the right choice. On a lightly loaded part, level B only adds cost. Choose the level according to the load and safety requirements of the part.

What happens if the drawing has no quality level?

Under EN 1090, the default level for the execution class applies (for example C for EXC2). Outside that scope, disputes between manufacturer and buyer are likely. We recommend always stating the level explicitly.

Can ISO 5817 be used for aluminium welds?

No. Aluminium and its alloys use EN ISO 10042. Its structure is similar to ISO 5817 and it defines the same levels B, C and D.

Does ISO 5817 cover internal imperfections?

Yes. It covers both surface and internal imperfections. Internal ones are found by RT or UT, and the matching acceptance level from ISO 17635 is used.
