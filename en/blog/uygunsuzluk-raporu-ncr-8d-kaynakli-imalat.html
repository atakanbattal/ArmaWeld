Nonconformance Reports (NCR) and 8D in Welded Fabrication: From Defect to Lasting Fix

2026-08-25 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

No fabricator works with zero defects; what makes the difference is how defects are handled. When European buyers assess a supplier, they don’t ask “have you ever made a mistake?” but “what did you do when you did?” This article explains how nonconformance reports (NCRs) and the 8D method work in welded fabrication.

What Is a Nonconformity?

A product failing to meet any requirement of the drawing, specification, standard or contract. Typical examples in welded fabrication:

  • An NDT indication beyond acceptance limits (crack, lack of fusion, porosity),
  • A part out of dimension or tolerance,
  • A missing or undersized weld,
  • Wrong material or missing certificate,
  • Deviation in surface preparation or paint thickness,
  • A weld made with the wrong WPS or by unqualified personnel.

What an NCR Should Contain

  • NCR number, date and originator,
  • Order, part number, revision and affected quantity,
  • Clear statement of requirement versus finding (photos, measurements, report numbers),
  • Where it was found: receiving, production, final inspection or customer,
  • Immediate action (segregating affected parts),
  • Disposition and who decided it,
  • Root cause and corrective action (with a link to an 8D if needed),
  • Close-out date and verification.

Disposition Options

DispositionMeaningNote
Use as isPart accepted without changeRequires a written customer concession if it deviates from a requirement
RepairMade usable though not fully meeting the requirementMay need a repair procedure and customer approval
ReworkCorrected to fully meet the requirementFor example gouging out and rewelding a defective weld
ScrapPart not usedA new part is made
Return to supplierDefect is in incoming material or partAn NCR is raised with the supplier

Weld repairs must be made to their own WPS and the repaired area must pass the same or stricter inspection (see weld defects and repair).

What Is 8D?

8D is an eight-step problem-solving method used especially for defects that reach the customer or recur. The goal is not just to fix the part but to make sure the defect does not happen again.

StepContent
D0Preparation: decide whether the problem needs an 8D
D1Team: welding coordinator, quality, production, design if needed
D2Problem description: what, where, when, how many, how detected
D3Containment: sorting parts in stock, in transit and at the customer
D4Root cause: why it happened and why it was not caught
D5Choosing permanent corrective actions
D6Implementation and verification of effectiveness
D7Prevention: extend to similar parts and processes, update documents
D8Closure and team recognition

Two Root Causes: Occurrence and Escape

A good 8D answers two questions separately: why did the defect occur, and why wasn’t it caught? For example, an undersized fillet weld may have occurred because of a wrong welder setting; but it reached the customer because no weld gauge was used at final inspection. Each needs its own action.

Typical Root Causes in Welding

  • Using a wrong or outdated WPS,
  • Parameters drifting outside the WPS range,
  • Deviation in joint preparation or root gap,
  • Damp consumables or insufficient preheat,
  • Misreading the weld symbol on the drawing,
  • Distortion from poor tacking or wrong weld sequence,
  • Lack of fusion in welds with poor access.

Root Cause Analysis Tools

5 Whys: asking “why?” repeatedly until reaching the real cause. “The welder made a mistake” is not a root cause; ask why the mistake was possible: missing instructions, wrong fixture, insufficient training?

Fishbone (Ishikawa): grouping possible causes under people, machine, material, method, measurement and environment. In welding, “environment” usually covers humidity, temperature and draughts.

Verifying Effectiveness

Corrective action must be shown to work with data: no recurrence in later batches, improved NDT results, dimensional reports within tolerance. An 8D closed without verification is not closed in the customer’s eyes.

Communicating with the Customer

  • Report the problem as soon as it is noticed, even before the root cause is known.
  • State containment and affected quantities clearly.
  • A short time is usually expected for the first steps (D1–D3) and a longer one for the full 8D; follow the times in the customer’s quality agreement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an NCR and an 8D?

An NCR records a single nonconformity and its disposition. 8D is an eight-step method to find root causes and lasting fixes, mainly for defects that reach the customer or recur.

When can “use as is” be decided?

When a deviation does not affect function and the customer has given a written concession.

Which root cause tools are used?

Most commonly the 5 Whys and the fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram.

When is an 8D closed?

After the effectiveness of corrective action is verified with data and extended to similar processes.
