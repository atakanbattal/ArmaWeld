Industrial Ductwork, Cyclones and Stacks: Sourcing Heavy-Gauge Welded Ducts

2026-05-11 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Thin galvanised building ventilation ducts are joined by seams and screws. Dust extraction, process air and flue gas ducts in industrial plants are mostly welded from 2–8 mm plate, sometimes thicker. They must withstand wear, temperature, pressure difference and vibration. Cyclones, hoppers and stacks belong to the same family.

Typical Products

  • Round and rectangular welded ducts, bends, reducers, branches.
  • Cyclone bodies and cones.
  • Steel stacks and stack connection pieces.
  • Damper bodies and explosion vent frames.
  • Duct supports and hanger structures.

Design and Material Decisions

  • Plate thickness: chosen by pressure difference, span and wear. With abrasive dust, the outer radius of bends wears fastest and gets thicker plate or wear lining.
  • Material: carbon steel for dry dust; stainless or coated steel in humid/corrosive service; heat-resistant steels at high temperature (see stainless fabrication).
  • Joints: angle or flat-bar flanges for site erection; flange hole patterns should be standard across the duct family.
  • Stiffening: external stiffeners on large rectangular ducts against panel vibration and collapse.

Welding and Tightness

Dust and gas ducts need continuous, tight seams. On thin sections, unmanaged heat input causes distortion and out-of-flat flanges. Tightness is usually checked visually and, where required, by pressure/vacuum or bubble testing. Welding galvanised sheet needs care for fumes and coating damage (see welding galvanised sheet).

For Site Erection

  • Parts are split by transport size and marked with erection numbers.
  • Make-up pieces can be left to match site dimensions.
  • Flange flatness is checked before painting.
  • Hanger points and lifting lugs are shown on the drawing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are industrial ducts welded?

Wear, temperature, pressure difference and tightness needs cannot be met with thin seamed sheet.

Where does a duct wear most?

With abrasive dust, on the outer radius of bends; thicker plate or lining goes there.

How is tightness checked?

Visually and, where needed, by pressure/vacuum or bubble testing.

Why standard flanges?

The same hole pattern across the duct family eases site erection and replacement.
