Weld Hardness Testing (HV10): Why It Is Done, Limits and Reading the Report

2026-09-22 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The heat-affected zone (HAZ), cooling quickly during welding, can become much harder and more brittle than the parent metal. Excessive hardness raises the risk of cold cracking, brittle fracture and, in some environments, stress corrosion. Hardness testing is the most practical way to show that a welding procedure keeps this risk under control.

How It Is Measured

Vickers hardness is measured on a macro section, usually with a 10 kgf load (HV10). ISO 9015-1 defines the traverses: along one or two lines just below the surface, points are taken through parent metal, HAZ and weld metal. The measurements must catch the hardest part of the HAZ close to the fusion line.

Limits

  • Procedure qualification: where hardness testing is required, ISO 15614-1 gives maximum values per material group; for common structural steels without heat treatment an upper limit such as 380 HV10 applies.
  • Sour service and hydrogen environments: oil and gas specifications usually require far lower limits, e.g. around 248–250 HV10.
  • Customer specification: some machine and crane OEMs set their own limits; clarify before quoting.

What Drives Hardness?

Carbon equivalent, plate thickness, heat input and preheat. Thick, high-CE material welded with low heat input and no preheat produces a hard HAZ. The remedies are correct preheat, adequate heat input, temper beads and, where needed, post-weld heat treatment.

Portable Hardness Testing

On a finished part, surface hardness can be checked without sectioning using portable devices (e.g. UCI or rebound). It is a quick screen but does not replace a laboratory HV10 traverse; surface preparation and calibration strongly affect the results.

What to Check in the Report

  • Load (HV10 or HV5) and a sketch of the traverses on the section.
  • The highest value in each zone and the limit applied.
  • Which WPQR or test coupon the measurement belongs to.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does HV10 mean?

Vickers hardness measured with a 10 kgf load.

Where is the hardest zone?

Usually the HAZ next to the fusion line.

How is hardness reduced?

With preheat, sufficient heat input, temper beads or heat treatment.

Is portable testing enough?

It is a screen; it does not replace a laboratory traverse.
