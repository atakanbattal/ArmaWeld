Weld Grinding: When Is It Needed, and When Does It Do Harm?

2026-06-15 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A ground weld often looks “higher quality”. But grinding is not a measure of quality; it is an extra operation that serves a specific purpose. Done in the right place, it improves fatigue life, makes inspection possible and helps paint adhere; done in the wrong place, it weakens the weld and raises cost unnecessarily.

When Grinding Is Needed

  • Non-destructive testing: in penetrant (PT) and magnetic particle (MT) testing an irregular surface can cause false indications; ultrasonic testing (UT) needs a smooth probe contact surface. The surface is then dressed only as far as the inspection requires (see NDT methods).
  • Fatigue-loaded joints: grinding the weld toe reduces stress concentration and can increase fatigue strength. If this improvement is used in design, the method, depth and inspection must be defined in the specification (see FAT classes).
  • Butt welds required flush: on mounting, sealing or flow surfaces the weld reinforcement is ground level with the surface.
  • Paint and coating preparation: at ISO 8501-3 grades P2 or P3, spatter, sharp edges and irregular profiles are removed (see P1–P3).
  • Hygienic design: in food and pharma equipment, internal welds may be ground and polished to a specified roughness for cleanability.
  • Defect repair: to remove an unacceptable defect before re-welding.

The Harm of Unnecessary Grinding

  • Reduced throat thickness: grinding a fillet weld can reduce the throat (a dimension), the load-bearing section. A fillet ground “to look nice” may end up below the size on the drawing (see throat thickness).
  • Undercut or notches: grinding into the parent metal can create a new notch at the weld toe.
  • Heat and discolouration: especially on stainless, grinding with heavy pressure heats the surface and can impair corrosion resistance.
  • Cost and time: grinding is a manual operation that can take as long as the welding itself. If the drawing does not require it, it inflates the quoted price needlessly.

Toe Grinding for Fatigue

Fatigue cracks usually start at the sharp transition and small flaws at the weld toe. Burr grinding aims to create a smooth radius at the toe and remove any micro-flaws. International Institute of Welding (IIW) recommendations advise grinding at least about 0.5 mm below any visible undercut, but no deeper than about 2 mm or about 7% of plate thickness in total. Grinding marks should be parallel to the stress direction; transverse marks create a new notch effect.

Grinding Direction and Surface

When butt welds are ground flush, the marks are left in the principal stress direction. Grinding depth into the parent metal on a flush butt weld is limited in most specifications so that it stays within the thickness tolerance. Inspecting the surface after grinding confirms that no defect has been hidden by it.

Care on Stainless Steel

  • Use only iron-free discs and brushes reserved for stainless; a disc used on carbon steel transfers iron onto the stainless surface.
  • Grinding does not replace pickling and passivation; the chromium-depleted layer beneath heat tint is removed more reliably by chemical treatment.

How to Specify It on the Drawing

If grinding is required, the drawing should state the surface shape (flat, convex, concave) together with the weld symbol and, where needed, the finishing method in a note. If it is unclear which welds are to be ground, for what purpose and to what extent, the shop either grinds nothing or needlessly grinds everything; both are unwanted outcomes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should welds be ground?

Only when there is a purpose: NDT preparation, fatigue improvement, a flush surface, paint preparation or hygiene. Without a purpose, grinding can weaken the weld and raise cost.

What happens if a fillet weld is ground?

The load-bearing throat thickness (a dimension) can drop below the value on the drawing. Cosmetic grinding of fillet welds should be avoided.

How is a weld toe ground for fatigue?

A smooth radius is formed at the toe with a rotary burr; depth is generally at least 0.5 mm below any undercut but no more than about 2 mm or 7% of thickness, with marks parallel to the stress direction.

Which discs should be used on stainless?

Only iron-free discs and brushes reserved for stainless. A disc used on carbon steel contaminates stainless with iron.
