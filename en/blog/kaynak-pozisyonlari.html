Welding Positions: EN ISO 6947 and AWS A3.0 Comparison

2026-03-27 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A welding position describes the orientation of the weld bead relative to gravity. A welder's qualification certificate (EN ISO 9606) and approved WPS (EN ISO 15614) cover specific positions.

EN ISO 6947 defines plate and pipe welding positions with internationally harmonised terminology. In the USA, AWS A3.0 uses different codes (1G, 2G, 3G, 4G, 5G, 6G).

Plate Positions

PositionDescriptionAWS Equivalent
PAFlat (downhand), weld on top surface1G / 1F
PBHorizontal fillet (T-joint, bead at 45°)2F
PCHorizontal butt (plate vertical, bead horizontal)2G
PDHorizontal overhead fillet (upward-facing T-joint)4F
PEOverhead (plate horizontal, bead welded from below)4G
PFVertical up (welding upwards)3G up
PGVertical down (welding downwards)3G down

Pipe Positions

PositionDescriptionAWS Equivalent
PA (pipe)Pipe horizontal, rotating — bead always at top1G pipe
PC (pipe)Pipe vertical, fixed — bead horizontal2G pipe
H-L045Pipe at 45°, fixed — combined position6G
PF (pipe)Pipe horizontal fixed, weld upward5G up
PG (pipe)Pipe horizontal fixed, weld downward5G down

Difficulty Ranking

Easy → Difficult:

  1. PA / PB — gravity assists the weld pool
  2. PC — pool fluidity difficult
  3. PF — preventing pool droop difficult but manageable
  4. PG — pool rides on slag, high risk of lack of fusion
  5. PD — overhead, gravity opposing
  6. PE — most difficult; pool must not drip
  7. H-L045 (6G) — combined; combines characteristics of all positions

EN ISO 9606 Qualification Range

  • PE position test — covers PA, PB, PC, PD, PE
  • PF position test — covers PA, PB, PF
  • PG position test — covers PG only
  • H-L045 pipe test — covers all fixed pipe positions

Practical recommendation: For site teams, the H-L045 (6G) test provides the broadest qualification. Pipe, pressure vessel and shipbuilding welders are typically certified to 6G.

Vertical Down (PG) Discussion

EN 1090-2 EXC3 and EXC4 structural welds largely prohibit PG due to the high risk of lack of side wall fusion. EN 13480 and EN 13445 also restrict PG use.
