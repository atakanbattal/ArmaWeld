Weld Strength Calculation: Fillet and Butt Welds to EN 1993-1-8

2026-02-05 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Weld sizes are often chosen by experience, but in load-bearing structures they need to be verified by calculation. In Europe, the reference for steel structures is EN 1993-1-8 (Eurocode 3, joints). This article explains the logic of fillet weld design, the two methods and a simple worked example. The aim is not to replace the designer, but to make clear where the weld size on a drawing comes from.

Key Quantities

  • a: fillet weld throat thickness (see our a dimension guide).
  • Lw: effective weld length. Ends that are not full size due to craters and starts are not counted.
  • fu: nominal ultimate tensile strength of the weaker parent material.
  • βw: correlation factor. EN 1993-1-8 gives 0.80 for S235, 0.85 for S275, 0.90 for S355 and 1.0 for S420 and S460.
  • γM2: partial safety factor for welds; the recommended value is 1.25 (the National Annex may differ).

Method 1: Simplified Method

The force the weld can carry per unit length is calculated regardless of load direction:

Fw,Rd = fvw,d × a, where fvw,d = (fu / √3) / (βw × γM2)

Condition: design force per unit length Fw,Ed ≤ Fw,Rd. This method is always on the safe side and quick for checks.

Method 2: Directional Method

The force is resolved into stresses on the throat plane: normal stress σ⊥, transverse shear τ⊥ and longitudinal shear τ∥. Both conditions must be met:

  • [σ⊥² + 3(τ⊥² + τ∥²)]0.5 ≤ fu / (βw × γM2)
  • σ⊥ ≤ 0.9 × fu / γM2

For loads transverse to the weld, the directional method can show up to about 20% more capacity than the simplified method. On large series, that can be enough to reduce the weld by one size.

Worked Example

An S355 flat bar is connected by two 200 mm fillet welds and carries a 300 kN design force parallel to the weld axis (eccentricity ignored).

  1. Taking fu = 490 MPa (minimum value in the material standard; check the value to use against the National Annex): fvw,d = (490 / 1.732) / (0.90 × 1.25) ≈ 251 MPa.
  2. Total effective length Lw = 2 × 200 = 400 mm.
  3. Required a = 300,000 N / (251 N/mm² × 400 mm) ≈ 3.0 mm.
  4. Allowing for end losses and fabrication margin, a4 is reasonable. Specifying a6 means about twice the required capacity and more than twice the weld metal.

Butt Welds

For full-penetration butt welds made with matching or overmatching consumables, the weld strength is taken as equal to the weaker parent material and no separate calculation is needed. Partial-penetration butt welds are designed like deep-penetration fillet welds; the effective throat is the penetration verified by procedure testing.

Long Joints

In lap joints longer than 150a, EN 1993-1-8 applies a reduction factor (βLw) because stress distribution is not uniform. Capacity does not grow linearly with very long welds.

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up a and z: calculating with z and writing it as a.
  • Ignoring eccentricity: in bracket joints the weld group also carries a moment.
  • Using the S355 βw value for higher-strength steels.
  • Relying on static design for dynamically loaded parts; fatigue is a separate check.
  • Mixing AWS D1.1 logic with Eurocode on US drawings. In AWS, allowable fillet weld stress is based on 0.30 times the filler metal strength.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which standard is used to calculate fillet weld strength?

In Europe, EN 1993-1-8 for steel structures. The simplified or directional method gives the force the weld can carry per unit length.

What is βw for S355?

Under EN 1993-1-8, the correlation factor for S355 is βw = 0.90.

Do full-penetration butt welds need calculating?

A full-penetration butt weld made with suitable consumables is taken as equal in strength to the weaker parent material; no separate weld calculation is needed.

Why does the directional method give more capacity?

It assesses the stress components separately, so it is less conservative than the simplified method, especially for transverse loads.
