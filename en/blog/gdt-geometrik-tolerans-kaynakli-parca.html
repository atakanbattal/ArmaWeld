GD&T on Welded Parts: How to Write Realistic Geometric Tolerances

2026-10-16 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Designers coming from machining tend to apply the same tight geometric tolerances to welded parts. Yet welding is a process that changes shape with heat. Well-written GD&T protects function while setting manufacturability and price. Badly written GD&T turns into a stream of rejections or unnecessary machining and straightening cost.

Start With Function

Every tolerance should rest on an assembly or functional need: what does this face touch? What is this hole bolted to? Non-functional faces can be left to ISO 13920 general tolerances. Tight tolerances belong only where truly needed.

Datum Selection

Datums should reflect how the part locates in assembly. On a welded part, a raw plate face or profile edge is often not a reliable datum; it distorts and waves. A machined mounting face or a pair of holes usually makes a better datum. Measurement and machining should use the same datums.

Common Tolerances and What Is Realistic in Welding

  • Flatness: on large welded faces, as-welded flatness is limited. If a mounting face needs a tight value, machine it after welding (see post-weld machining).
  • Perpendicularity and parallelism: well controlled with fixtures, but angular distortion accumulates on long arms.
  • Position: holes made before welding shift during welding. Holes with tight position tolerance should be drilled or machined after welding.
  • Profile: used on complex contours; depends on cutting accuracy and may change after welding.

Before or After Welding?

The drawing should state at which stage a dimension is achieved. Faces machined after welding get a machining allowance and are shown separately. Otherwise the shop tries to hold a dimension by welding that should be machined, and results vary.

Specifications That Add Cost for Nothing

  • One tight general tolerance for the whole drawing.
  • Flatness on non-functional outer faces.
  • Tight position on holes that will not be machined after welding.
  • A raw, distortion-prone face chosen as datum.

Talk to the Supplier Early

A supplier who reviews tolerances at quotation will propose which dimensions are held by welding and which by machining. That conversation prevents rejections and disputes in series production.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should welded parts carry GD&T?

Yes, but only on functional faces and holes, with realistic values.

Can a raw plate face be a datum?

Often not reliably; machined faces or holes make better datums.

When are tight-position holes made?

After welding; holes made before shift during welding.

How do you reduce tolerance cost?

Return to general tolerances where there is no function.
