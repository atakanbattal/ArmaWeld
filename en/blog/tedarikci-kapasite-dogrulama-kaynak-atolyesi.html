How to Verify a Welding Supplier's Capacity: A Practical Guide for Buyers

2025-12-02 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

"We have capacity" is the most frequently heard and least verified sentence in supplier meetings. A welding workshop's capacity is measured neither in square metres nor in number of machines. The real question is: can your part be made at your quality, at your rate, alongside the work already in the shop?

Why Capacity Is Misjudged

  • Machines are counted, but nobody asks how many certified welders can run them.
  • Floor area is seen, but crane capacity and hook height are not checked.
  • Welding capacity is reviewed, while cutting, painting or NDT bottlenecks are missed.
  • Today's free space is noticed, but the load three months from now is not asked.

Find the Bottleneck

The flow of a welded part usually runs through cutting, bending, tacking and welding, straightening, NDT, surface treatment and shipping. The real capacity of the workshop equals the slowest link in that chain. Even with a free welding bay, the paint line or the queue at the galvanising partner can decide the delivery date. Asking the supplier for the process steps of your part and the typical duration of each makes the bottleneck visible (see lead time planning).

Rough Calculation: How Much Do You Need?

Go into the capacity discussion with your own numbers. A simple approach:

  1. Estimate the welding time per part (weld length, thickness, process; see welding cost calculation).
  2. Add an allowance for preparation, tacking, grinding and handling.
  3. Multiply by the monthly quantity and convert to welder-hours.
  4. Compare this with the supplier's number of relevant certified welders and shift pattern.

The calculation is not exact, but it shows how much of the supplier's total capacity your work would take. If that share is very high, a single breakdown or another large order will hit you directly.

Ask for Evidence

  • Photos and references of parts of similar size and weight.
  • Recent on-time delivery (OTD) data (see supplier performance KPIs).
  • Welder certificate list and WPQR coverage.
  • Typical lead times with outsourced process partners.

Flexibility Questions

How capacity behaves when demand changes matters as much as today's figure. What happens if your demand rises 30%? Which jobs can be moved for an urgent batch? Are people available for an extra shift? Can material be stocked? Concrete answers separate a realistic supplier from an over-optimistic one.

Warning Signs

  • Generic answers like "we can handle anything" to the load question.
  • The number of welders does not match the certificate list.
  • Dependence on a single partner for outsourced processes, with no lead time information.
  • Most of the area shown during a site visit is empty or allocated to other work (see site visit checklist).

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a welding workshop's capacity measured?

By combining the number of welders certified for your process, the shift pattern, equipment, cranes and space, outsourced process lead times and current load.

Why does the bottleneck matter?

The workshop's real output equals the slowest link in the chain from cutting to painting.

Which documents should I ask the supplier for?

Welder certificate list, WPQR coverage, references for similar parts, on-time delivery data and outsourced process lead times.

How do I calculate my own need?

Add a preparation allowance to the welding time per part and multiply by monthly quantity to get welder-hours.
