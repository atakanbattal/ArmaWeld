Charpy Impact Testing: How to Prove Weld Toughness at Low Temperature

2026-09-28 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

As temperature falls, steel at some point changes from ductile behaviour to brittle fracture. For a crane working outdoors in Scandinavia, a machine used on site in winter or a chassis under impact loads, the real question is not "how strong" but "how tough in the cold". The measure for that is the Charpy V-notch impact test.

How the Test Works

A standard 10×10×55 mm specimen is V-notched, cooled to the required temperature (e.g. 0, -20, -40 °C) and broken by a pendulum in one blow. The absorbed energy is read in joules. Usually the average of three specimens and a minimum single value are assessed together.

Steel Grades and the Weld

The suffixes in the S355 family describe impact values: JR 27 J at 20 °C, J0 at 0 °C, J2 at -20 °C; K2 40 J at -20 °C; NL is guaranteed down to -50 °C (see S355 grades). These values apply to the parent metal. Weld metal and HAZ can behave very differently depending on welding conditions, so specifications may require separate impact tests for the weld.

Where Is the Notch? (ISO 9016)

  • Weld metal (VWT): toughness of filler and procedure.
  • Fusion line / HAZ (VHT): often the critical zone; coarse-grained HAZ can lose toughness.

The depth below the surface at which specimens are taken is also stated in the report.

What Affects Weld Toughness?

  • Heat input: excessive heat input causes grain growth and loss of toughness in the HAZ; staying within the qualified range matters.
  • Interpass temperature: too high has a similar effect.
  • Filler metal: choose wire or electrodes with guaranteed low-temperature toughness.
  • Thickness and bead sequence: in multi-pass welds later beads partly temper earlier ones.

What Should the Buyer Clarify?

  • Test temperature and energy requirement (e.g. 27 J at -20 °C) for both parent metal and weld.
  • Whether a WPQR meeting this requirement exists.
  • Impact values shown on the 3.1 material certificate.
  • How heat input will be recorded in production.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Charpy test measure?

The energy needed to break a notched specimen at a given temperature, i.e. toughness.

Is S355J2 enough for the weld?

The steel guarantees 27 J at -20 °C; the weld may need separate verification.

Which zone is most critical?

Usually the fusion line and coarse-grained HAZ.

Why does heat input matter?

Too much heat input causes grain growth and lower toughness.
