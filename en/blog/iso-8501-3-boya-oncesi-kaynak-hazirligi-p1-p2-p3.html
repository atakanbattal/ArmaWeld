ISO 8501-3 P1, P2, P3: How to Prepare Welds and Edges Before Painting

2026-11-16 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

On painted steel structures, corrosion often starts not on flat surfaces but at welds, sharp edges and cut faces. Paint thins out on a sharp edge, leaves voids under spatter and cannot enter a pore. ISO 8501-3 defines how far these imperfections must be removed before painting with three preparation grades: P1, P2 and P3.

A P Grade Is Not the Same as Sa 2½

Two concepts are often confused. ISO 8501-1 (Sa 2, Sa 2½, Sa 3) describes how clean the surface is from rust and mill scale after blasting. ISO 8501-3 defines the fabrication preparation done before blasting, i.e. removing weld, edge and surface imperfections by grinding and dressing. A coating specification usually states both, for example “P2, Sa 2½”.

What the Grades Mean

  • P1, light preparation: only the most basic corrections before painting. Loose spatter and slag are removed; many imperfections stay as they are.
  • P2, thorough preparation: most imperfections are removed. Sharp edges are rounded, adhering spatter removed, open pores made accessible to paint or ground out.
  • P3, very thorough preparation: the surface is free of visible imperfections. Edges are rounded to a radius of at least 2 mm, weld ripple is dressed, and no spatter or visible pores remain.

Which Grade to Choose?

EN 1090-2 links the preparation grade to the expected life of the coating system and the corrosivity category to ISO 12944. The general trend:

  • Indoor, low corrosivity (C1): P1 is usually enough.
  • Medium corrosivity (C2, C3): P2 is the common choice.
  • High corrosivity (C4, C5, CX) with long life expectancy: P3 is required.

The exact grade should be stated in the specification or on the drawing. If it is missing, clarifying it at quotation avoids later disputes, because P3 means considerably more grinding labour than P1.

Cost Impact and Design Measures

Preparation time scales with weld length and edge length. The way to cut cost is to reduce imperfections at source rather than grind them later:

  • Design edges rounded at cutting or bending
  • Welding parameters and shielding gas that reduce spatter (see MIG/MAG parameters)
  • Continuous welds, which are easier to coat than intermittent welds
  • Avoid narrow gaps and pockets paint cannot reach

Frequently Asked Questions

What does ISO 8501-3 P2 mean?

Thorough preparation before painting: sharp edges are rounded, adhering spatter removed, and sharp irregularities, undercut and open pores dealt with.

What edge radius does P3 require?

In P3 preparation, edges are rounded to a radius of at least 2 mm.

What is the difference between Sa 2½ and P2?

Sa 2½ (ISO 8501-1) defines surface cleanliness after blasting; P2 (ISO 8501-3) defines removal of weld and edge imperfections before blasting. Both are specified together.

Which P grade should I choose?

Per EN 1090-2 it depends on coating life and ISO 12944 corrosivity category: P1 for low corrosivity, P2 for medium, P3 for severe conditions and long life.
