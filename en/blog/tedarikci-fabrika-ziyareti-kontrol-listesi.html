Visiting a Welding Supplier: What to Look at in One Day

2026-01-02 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

You can get to know a welding shop from presentations and certificates, but the real picture is on the shop floor. A site visit does not need to be as comprehensive as a formal audit; a well-prepared tour of a few hours tells you much about the supplier's daily reality that documents cannot.

Before the Visit

  • Share your part drawing and critical points with the supplier in advance; ask to come on a day when similar work is in production.
  • List the documents you want to see: WPS/WPQR list, welder certificates, NDT personnel certificates, calibration records.
  • Be clear about the purpose: pre-selection, capacity check or a review after a problem?

Questions to Ask

  • "If you were making this part, which WPS would you use?" The speed and accuracy of the answer shows how strong welding coordination is.
  • "What was your biggest quality problem last quarter and how did you solve it?" An open answer is a sign of a mature quality culture.
  • "Which processes do you outsource, and how do you control your partners?"
  • "Who decides on straightening and repair after welding?"
  • "How are documents and reports sent to the customer?"

Talk to a Welder

The most instructive part of a visit is often a short conversation with a welder. Ask which part they are working on, which parameters they use, and whom they tell when they see a problem. A shop where the welder knows the drawing and the WPS shows that what management says is also practised on the floor (see welder certification).

Warning Signs

  • Consumables left open in a damp environment.
  • WPSs only in a binder in the office.
  • Measuring tools without calibration labels or with expired ones.
  • Non-conforming parts mixed with good parts.
  • Much of the capacity shown consists of idle or unused equipment (see capacity verification).

After the Visit

Write up your observations the same day and share them with the supplier. Ask for a dated correction plan for any gaps. For critical parts the visit should be followed by a fuller supplier audit or first article approval (see supplier audits).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a site visit the same as a supplier audit?

No. A visit is shorter and based on observation; an audit is a structured, standards-based assessment that includes record review.

What is the most important observation during a visit?

Whether documents such as WPS, certificates and inspection records are actually used on the floor.

Why talk to a welder?

A welder who knows the drawing and WPS shows that management's statements are practised on the floor.

What should happen after the visit?

Write up and share observations the same day and ask for a dated correction plan for gaps.
