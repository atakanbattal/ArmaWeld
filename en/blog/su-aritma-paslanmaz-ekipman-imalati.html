Water and Wastewater Treatment Equipment Fabrication: Stainless Choice, Weld Cleaning and Common Mistakes

2026-08-10 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Treatment equipment makers usually subcontract welded parts such as screens, grit and grease traps, clarifier scraper bridges, launders, tanks and pipe spools. These parts spend years in contact with water, sludge and chlorides. Most corrosion problems seen on site come not from the material but from welding and cleaning workmanship.

Material: 304L or 316L?

  • 304L: common in clean water and low-chloride environments.
  • 316L: molybdenum improves resistance to chlorides and pitting; preferred for wastewater, coastal plants and chemical dosing areas.
  • Galvanised carbon steel: an economical option for supporting frames, platforms and railings above water.

The choice belongs to the equipment maker and project specification; the fabricator uses the right grade with material certificates (see stainless fabrication).

Weld Quality

  • Root purging: back purging on pipes and closed sections prevents root oxidation.
  • Continuous welds: intermittent welds and tight gaps between overlapping plates start crevice corrosion; prefer continuous welds in wet zones.
  • Heat input: controlled TIG heat input on thin sheet reduces distortion and discolouration (see TIG welding stainless).

Post-Weld Cleaning

Heat tint lowers corrosion resistance. Pickling removes the oxide layer, passivation restores the protective chromium oxide film (see pickling and passivation). Bath, gel or electrolytic methods are chosen by part size.

Carbon Steel Contamination

If stainless is worked on the same bench, with the same grinding disc or brush as carbon steel, iron particles stick to the surface and rust spots soon follow. Use separate tools, racks and areas for stainless. A large share of "the stainless is rusting" complaints start here.

Large Parts and Transport

Scraper bridges and long launders are split for transport and bolted on site. Fasteners should also be stainless; consider galvanic corrosion where galvanised bolts touch stainless parts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which stainless for wastewater equipment?

Usually 316L with chlorides and chemicals; 304L is common in clean water.

Why pickle after welding?

Heat tint lowers corrosion resistance; pickling and passivation restore protection.

Why does stainless rust?

Mostly carbon steel contamination, uncleaned heat tint or crevice corrosion.

Must frames be stainless too?

Above water, galvanised carbon steel is an economical option.
