Subcontract Welding Agreement: 15 Clauses to Settle Before the Order

2026-07-07 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Most disputes in subcontract welded fabrication are not technical; they come from ambiguity: which revision applies, who pays for inspection, who does the painting, what delay is measured against. This article lists the points a framework agreement or purchase specification should settle, seen from the fabricator’s side.

Note: this article is general information, not legal advice. Prepare your contract with a lawyer.

1. Scope and Drawing Revision

Which part numbers, drawing revisions and quantities does the order cover? The revision number should be stated on the order. Wording like “latest revision” makes it unclear who is right when a change arrives during production.

2. Who Supplies the Material?

Is material supplied by the fabricator or the customer? For free-issue material, state the scrap allowance, ownership of offcuts, responsibility for missing or defective material and who provides the EN 10204 certificates.

3. Quality Requirements

  • Execution class (e.g. EN 1090-2 EXC2 or EXC3),
  • ISO 5817 quality level (B, C or D),
  • EN ISO 3834 level,
  • Revision of any customer-specific welding specification.

4. Inspection and Testing

Settle the NDT method and extent (e.g. 10% UT, 100% VT), inspector level, report format and the cost of re-inspection. If additional inspection is requested by the customer and finds no defect, who pays? Answer that in advance.

5. ITP, Hold Points and First Article

At which stages is customer witnessing required (hold point), and where is notification enough (witness point)? Is there a first article inspection (FAI), and how long does approval take? See the ITP guide. Also state what happens if the customer does not attend a hold point on time.

6. Tolerances

Which general tolerance applies to dimensions not toleranced on the drawing? Without an ISO 13920 class (A, B, C, D and E, F, G, H), a dimensional argument is inevitable.

7. Who Does Surface Treatment?

Is blasting, painting, galvanizing or passivation in the fabricator’s scope or the customer’s? If the fabricator coordinates it, state the system, thickness and corrosivity category (ISO 12944). The weld preparation grade before painting (ISO 8501-3 P1, P2, P3) belongs here too.

8. Documentation

Which documents come with delivery: material certificates, WPS and WPQR list, welder certificates, NDT reports, dimensional report, declaration of conformity, data book? Is delivery complete if documents are missing?

9. Packing, Delivery and Incoterms

Delivery terms should be written with Incoterms 2020 (EXW, FCA, DAP etc.). Packing standard, pallet type, corrosion protection and labelling are part of the contract too.

10. Lead Time and Delay

When does the lead time start: order date, drawing approval or arrival of customer material? If there is a delay penalty, state its cap and the definition of force majeure. Customer-caused delays (late approval, late material) should be excluded from the calculation.

11. Change Management

How is a revision received during production handled? Define the written change request, approval of price and lead-time impact, and the status of parts already made to the old revision.

12. Nonconformity (NCR) and Costs

State the notification period, the fabricator’s right to repair or remake, approval of the repair procedure, and who bears removal, refitting and transport costs. Liability is usually capped at the part price or order value.

13. Warranty and Liability

Warranty period, scope (material, workmanship, design) and whether indirect losses are excluded. If the design comes from the customer, design errors are not the fabricator’s responsibility; state this distinction clearly.

14. Confidentiality, IP and Fixtures

Settle drawing confidentiality (NDA), the terms for sharing drawings with third parties (sub-suppliers) and ownership of special fixtures or tooling. If the customer paid for a fixture, it is the customer’s; state storage, maintenance and return terms.

15. Payment, Price Adjustment and Law

  • Payment terms and advance,
  • An index or threshold for steel price changes (e.g. re-pricing if material cost moves beyond a set percentage),
  • Currency,
  • Governing law, whether the CISG applies to international sales, and arbitration or court venue.

Practical Tip

You do not have to fit everything into one contract. A common structure: a framework agreement with general terms, a technical specification per part, and a purchase order with quantity, price and date. What matters is stating which document prevails in case of conflict.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which clause is most often missing?

Usually who does surface treatment, inspection costs and the drawing revision. Leaving them out leads to disputes at delivery.

When should lead time start?

Usually from drawing approval and the date all required material or information is complete. Customer-caused delays should be excluded.

Who owns the fixture?

The customer, if the customer paid for it. Storage, maintenance and return terms should be in the contract.

What if the steel price changes?

If an index or threshold is defined, the price is updated accordingly; otherwise the fixed price applies.
