Pipe Support Fabrication: Many Items, Small Quantities, Tight Tracking

2026-04-28 6 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

A process plant, power station or industrial facility project contains hundreds, sometimes thousands of pipe supports. Individually they are simple welded parts: a shoe, a trunnion, a guide, an L bracket. The challenge is not the part itself but marking many different items correctly, making them from the right material and getting them to site in the right order.

Typical Support Types

  • Shoe: welded or clamped under the pipe, resting on a beam.
  • Trunnion: a pipe stub off an elbow used for support.
  • Guides and anchors: parts restricting pipe movement.
  • Brackets and frames: L, T and portal supports off columns or walls.
  • Pipe rack parts: rack beams and secondary members.

Standard Catalogue and Marking

Projects usually define standard support types in a catalogue and assign support numbers on isometrics. The fabricator's most important input is a clean list with type, dimensions, quantity and material for each support number. Each part should be permanently marked with its support number (hard stamp, tag or paint code) that stays legible after galvanising or painting.

Material and Traceability

Alongside carbon steel supports, there are pads (wear pads, repads) touching stainless or low-temperature steel pipes. Parts welded to the pipe must match the pipe material and have traceable certificates (often EN 10204 3.1) (see EN 10204 3.1). Carbon steel must not touch stainless pipe directly.

Welding and Surface

  • Supports mostly use fillet welds; throat sizes are in the catalogue.
  • Pads welded to the pipe follow the piping weld procedure and preheat if needed.
  • Hot-dip galvanising is common outdoors, paint indoors.

Delivery and Documents

Supports going to site should be packed and labelled by area or line. Material certificates, weld records, paint/galvanising reports and packing lists are usually collected in a manufacturing record book (see MDR).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the biggest challenge in pipe support fabrication?

Correctly marking many different items, using the right material and shipping in the right order.

What matters for pads welded to the pipe?

Material matching the pipe, traceable certificates and the piping weld procedure.

How are supports marked?

With the support number, using permanent marking legible after galvanising or painting.

Galvanised or painted?

Mostly galvanised outdoors and painted indoors; the project specification decides.
