Design for Welding (DFM): 10 Rules That Set Price and Quality on the Drawing

2026-10-29 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Most of a welded part's cost is set on the drawing board long before the first spark. The number, size, accessibility and distortion tendency of welds are decided in design. The rules below are a checklist for buyers and designers before a drawing goes to a supplier.

1. Think About Weld Access

The torch or electrode must reach the joint at the right angle. Welds in tight corners, between nested plates or inside closed boxes are either impossible or poor. Checking in the 3D model with the torch body in mind is a good start.

2. Size Welds by Calculation

Doubling the throat size of a fillet weld roughly quadruples weld metal volume. Oversized welds "to be safe" increase both cost and distortion.

3. Reduce the Number of Welds

A bent sheet can replace two plates welded together. A contour laser-cut in one piece is cheaper and cleaner than welding several small parts.

4. Build in Symmetry

Welds balanced around the neutral axis offset each other's distortion. Heavy welding on one side pulls the part that way (see weld distortion).

5. Avoid Intersecting Welds

Points where three welds meet concentrate stress and defects. Corner copes (scallops) separating the welds improve quality and fatigue life.

6. Keep Away From High-Stress Zones

Move welds away from sections of maximum bending stress and abrupt changes in section where possible. On fatigue-loaded parts this directly affects the FAT class.

7. Use Standard Material and Sections

Readily available plate thicknesses and sections shorten lead time. A special thickness or rare grade can hold up the whole order for one part.

8. Allow Flat-Position Welding

Flat and horizontal positions are fastest and give the best quality. If the part can be turned over or rotated on a positioner, overhead and vertical welding is reduced.

9. Tolerance by Function

Leave tight dimensions that welding cannot hold to post-weld machining and define machining allowance. On non-functional faces, ISO 13920 general tolerances are enough.

10. Plan Surface Treatment From the Start

Drain holes in closed sections going to galvanising or e-coat, seal welds or gaps at laps, and rounded edges for painting belong in the design.
