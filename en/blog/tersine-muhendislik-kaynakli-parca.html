Reverse Engineering Welded Parts: Making a Part Correctly Without a Drawing

2026-09-14 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

The frame of an old machine has cracked, its manufacturer no longer exists and you have no drawing. Or you want a second source for a load-bearing part used for years, but the drawing is out of date. The answer is reverse engineering: creating a manufacturable technical package from the existing part.

When Is It Needed?

  • Spare parts for machines whose maker has closed or ended support,
  • Parts whose drawing is lost or that were heavily modified in the field,
  • An improved version of a part with recurring failures,
  • Turning paper drawings into digital, manufacturable ones.

Intellectual Property

Before starting, make sure you have the right to reproduce the part. Spare parts for your own machine are usually fine; if there is design protection or a contractual restriction, get legal advice. The fabricator may ask for a written statement from the customer.

1. Measuring

MethodSuitsNote
Hand tools (tape, calliper, protractor)Simple, prismatic partsCheap and fast, limited for complex shapes
Measuring from datums and gaugesHole axes, interface pointsCritical dimensions for assembly function
3D scanningComplex, curved or large partsUsually done with a specialist partner
Measuring the mating partWhen the host machine is availableGives real assembly dimensions

The key is to separate the part as it is today from the part as designed. A used part may be worn, distorted or repaired in the field. A measured value is not always the design value.

2. Identifying the Material

  • Chemical analysis (spectrometer / PMI): helps identify the steel grade; usually done by an accredited lab or a partner with a mobile device.
  • Hardness testing: gives an idea of strength class and heat treatment.
  • Thickness measurement: ultrasonic thickness testing on corroded parts.

Chemical analysis gives an approximate structural steel grade, but impact toughness (such as J2 or K2) cannot be determined from analysis. So the new part’s material should be chosen deliberately for the application (see S355 sub-grades).

3. Analysing the Welds

  • Weld type and size (fillet throat a, full or partial penetration for butt welds),
  • Joint preparation (from cross-sections or edge marks),
  • Traces of the welding process (slag, spatter, bead appearance),
  • A macro section from a scrapped sample if needed.

Full penetration cannot always be seen from outside. For a load-bearing joint, if in doubt, designing for full penetration and choosing the WPS accordingly keeps you on the safe side.

4. What Cannot Be Recovered

Reverse engineering cannot bring everything back. These are usually unknown or need assumptions:

  • Original tolerances and which dimensions are functional,
  • Original design loads and safety factors,
  • Any stress-relief heat treatment applied,
  • Original inspection scope and quality level.

These assumptions must be written into the drawing and quote and approved by the customer.

5. A Chance to Improve

If the part keeps cracking in the same place, an exact copy will fail the same way. Reverse engineering is a good chance to move weld toes away from high-stress zones, soften sharp corners or upgrade the material grade (see fatigue in welded joints).

6. Technical Package

  • Dimensioned production drawing with weld symbols,
  • Bill of materials and material grades,
  • 3D model where needed,
  • Welding and inspection requirements,
  • List of assumptions.

7. Verification

The first part is made with a dimensional report and, where possible, fitted to the machine for a function test. After approval the drawing revision is frozen and later production follows that revision (see first article approval).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a part without a drawing be made?

Yes. The existing part is measured, material and welds are analysed, a production drawing is prepared and verified with a first article.

How is the material grade found?

Chemical analysis and hardness give an indication, but properties like impact toughness cannot be determined, so the new material is chosen for the application.

Are measurements from a worn part accurate?

Not always. Wear, distortion and field repairs must be considered; measure the mating part where possible.

Is copying the part legal?

Spare parts for your own machine are usually fine; get legal advice if there is design protection or a contractual restriction.
