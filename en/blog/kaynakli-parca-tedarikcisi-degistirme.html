Changing Your Welded Parts Supplier: How to Transfer Production Without Disruption

2025-11-20 8 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

On paper, changing the supplier of a welded part looks simple: send the drawing, get a price, place the order. In practice, a lot of knowledge builds up at the old supplier over the years and never makes it onto a drawing. The welding sequence, a small pre-set in the fixture, a quiet correction to a hole that caused trouble in assembly. A transfer plan exists to bring this unwritten knowledge to the surface.

Why Companies Change Suppliers

  • Delivery performance keeps slipping or capacity is no longer enough.
  • Recurring quality problems and corrective actions that never close.
  • Cost pressure, currency or energy-driven price increases.
  • Reducing dependence on a single source (see dual sourcing strategy).
  • The old supplier stops the business or exits the product group.

Whatever the reason, the customer's line must not stop during the transfer. That is why the schedule, the technical handover and the stock plan are run together.

1. Assemble the Technical Data Pack

Sending only a PDF drawing to the new supplier is the most common mistake. A complete pack contains:

DocumentWhy it matters
Current drawing revision and 3D modelCutting and bending programs are built from the model
Material and certificate requirementFor example S355J2 with EN 10204 3.1
Weld class and quality levelISO 5817 level, EN 1090 execution class
Inspection plan and NDT ratesWhich method on which weld, at what percentage
Surface treatment specificationBlast grade, paint system, thickness
Recent NCRs and customer complaintsPrevents known problems from repeating
Packaging and labelling instructionsHow the line expects to receive parts

If the relationship with the old supplier is still workable, ask for the welding sequence, fixture photos and measurement reports too. They shorten the new supplier's learning curve considerably.

2. Who Owns the Fixtures, Tools and Gauges?

Customer-owned welding fixtures, check gauges and special tooling are recovered according to the contract and sent to the new supplier. Photograph and measure them before shipment; starting with a damaged fixture means a non-conformity on the first part. If the fixture is not customer property, design and manufacture of a new one goes into the plan (see welding fixture design).

3. Welding Procedures and Qualifications

A WPS belongs to the manufacturer; the old supplier's WPS cannot simply be used in a new workshop. The new supplier needs approved WPQRs covering the required material, thickness and position range, and matching welder certificates. Where something is missing, new procedure qualification takes time and must be in the schedule (see What is a WPS?).

4. First Article Approval

The first parts from the new supplier should go through full dimensional inspection, weld inspection and material document review before series production. In automotive and similar sectors this runs through PPAP; elsewhere a first article report is usually enough (see first article approval). A trial assembly on the customer's line reveals fit problems a dimensional report cannot show.

5. Parallel Phase and Safety Stock

The riskiest part of a transfer is the gap between the old supplier stopping and the new one reaching full rate. Two practical measures help:

  • Safety stock: extra parts from the old supplier, enough to cover the transition.
  • Parallel production: for a period both suppliers make the same part, and the new supplier's share is raised step by step.

Size the buffer on the time needed for first article approval plus one possible correction loop. Assuming everything will pass at the first attempt is an optimistic plan.

6. Example Timeline

PhaseContent
PreparationData pack, quotation, audit or site visit
Technical handoverFixture transfer or build, WPS check, material procurement
First articleSample production, measurement, inspection, trial assembly
Parallel phaseSmall batches, performance tracking
Full transferOld supplier closed out, safety stock consumed

The duration of each phase depends on part complexity, material lead time and fixture needs, so the schedule should be written together with the supplier using real lead times.

Most Common Mistakes

  • Requesting quotes on an outdated drawing revision.
  • Hiding known problems from the new supplier and then seeing the same defect again.
  • Comparing unit price only, ignoring fixture, freight and approval costs.
  • Closing the old supplier without a safety stock.
  • Approving the first article on a dimensional report alone, without a trial assembly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to change a welded parts supplier?

It depends on the part; data pack, fixture needs, material lead time and first article approval have to be planned together.

Can the old supplier's WPS be used by the new supplier?

No. WPS and WPQR belong to the manufacturer; the new workshop must produce under its own approved procedures.

How do I avoid a line stop during the transfer?

Build a safety stock from the old supplier and run a period of parallel production.

Which documents should I give the new supplier?

Current drawing and 3D model, material and quality requirements, inspection plan, surface specification, known problems and packaging instructions.
