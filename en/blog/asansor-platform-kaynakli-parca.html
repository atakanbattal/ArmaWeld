Welded Fabrication of Lift and Load Platform Parts

2026-09-25 7 min read ArmaWeld Engineering

Lifts and lifting platforms carry people and goods, so both strength and dimensional accuracy are critical in their load-bearing welded parts. A few millimetres of angular deviation in a car frame can stop the guide shoes from sitting properly on the rail, causing noise and early wear. This article summarises what matters in welding lift and platform parts.

Typical Welded Parts

  • Lift car frame (sling: crosshead, bottom beam, uprights),
  • Car floor frame and load platform chassis,
  • Counterweight frame,
  • Guide rail brackets and fixing brackets,
  • Machine bedplates and drive unit supports,
  • Scissor arms and base frames of scissor lifting tables,
  • Masts and guide sections of platform lifts.

Which legislation applies, design calculations, type examination and final tests are the responsibility of the lift or platform manufacturer. The welding shop’s job is to meet every requirement on the drawing and prove it with records (see the new Machinery Regulation).

Dimensional Accuracy

General tolerances are often not enough for lift parts; functional dimensions are specified separately on the drawing:

  • Parallelism and spacing of guide shoe mounting faces,
  • Diagonal difference and squareness of the car frame,
  • Flatness of the floor frame,
  • Centre distance and parallelism of pin holes in scissor arms.

Holding these takes fixtures, weld sequence and, where needed, machining after welding, planned together. If bores and mounting faces are machined after welding, the effect of weld distortion is removed. For general tolerances, the ISO 13920 class should be stated on the drawing.

Weld Quality

  • Load-bearing joints are usually specified at ISO 5817 level C, critical joints at B (see ISO 5817).
  • Welders qualified to ISO 9606-1 and procedures to ISO 15614-1.
  • ISO 3834-3 is commonly expected for welding quality management.

Fatigue

Lifts and platforms see many load cycles every day. Scissor arms, pin bearings and corner joints of car frames are especially fatigue-sensitive. Keeping weld toes away from high-stress areas, rounding corners and filling weld craters extends life noticeably (see fatigue and FAT classes).

Inspection

  • Visual and dimensional inspection of all welds,
  • MT or PT on critical joints,
  • UT on thick butt welds,
  • Dimensional report for functional dimensions.

Surface Protection

Guide rail brackets and counterweight frames in the shaft are usually hot-dip galvanised or painted. Car frames are mostly powder coated. For outdoor platforms, a system suited to the corrosivity category should be chosen (see ISO 12944 corrosivity categories).

Documentation

  • EN 10204 3.1 material certificates and traceability,
  • WPS, WPQR and welder certificates,
  • Inspection reports and dimensional report,
  • Serial number or batch label.

These records show the part’s conformity in the manufacturer’s technical file and in any audit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which standards apply to lift parts?

EN 81-20 and EN 81-50 for passenger and goods passenger lifts; EN 81-41 for platforms for persons with impaired mobility; EN 1570-1 for scissor lifting tables. The product manufacturer decides which applies.

Why are dimensions so important in a car frame?

Guide shoes only sit properly on the rail if parallelism and squareness are right; deviations cause noise and early wear.

When is machining after welding needed?

For tight-tolerance features such as pin holes and mounting faces, to remove the effect of weld distortion.

Who carries out load tests?

Design verification and final tests are the lift or platform manufacturer’s responsibility.
