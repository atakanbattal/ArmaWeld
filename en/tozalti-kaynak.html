Submerged Arc Welding Services

The right process for long, heavy seams

In submerged arc welding the arc burns beneath a layer of granular flux. High deposition, deep penetration and a smooth bead mean fewer passes and fewer stop-start points than MAG on long, heavy-section joints.

Linear Welding

Linear long-seam welding

Girders, box sections, plate butt joints and tank shells: the 35 m machine length lets long seams be welded in one set-up at constant speed and parameters. Fewer start and stop points mean fewer defects.

Boom Welding

Boom welding

Corner and butt seams on the box sections of excavator, crane and aerial platform booms. Booms under fatigue loading get continuous, uniform seams with a controlled welding sequence and distortion monitoring.

Chassis Welding

Chassis welding

Long side members and I/box-section carriers for trailers, semi-trailers, agricultural and construction machinery chassis. Straightness and dimensional repeatability over the full length come first.

FAQ

Submerged arc welding FAQ

What is submerged arc welding?

Submerged arc welding (SAW, ISO 4063-121) is an automated arc process in which the arc between a continuously fed wire and the workpiece burns beneath granular flux. The flux shields the arc and weld pool and gives high deposition and deep penetration.

What does a 35 m SAW machine offer?

Long parts can be welded in a single set-up. Speed and parameters stay constant along the seam, so quality is repeatable, stop-start points and their defect risk drop, and repositioning time is cut.

Which parts suit submerged arc welding?

Medium and heavy steel parts with long, straight seams that can be welded flat (PA) or as horizontal fillets (PB): booms, chassis members, welded I-girders, box columns, plate butt joints and tank shells. MAG is the better choice for very short seams and thin sheet.

Do you offer subcontract SAW?

Yes. We weld booms, chassis and long-seam parts for your products to your drawings. Parts can come in for welding only, or be quoted as complete fabrication including cutting, bending, welding and NDT.

What do you need for a quote?

A drawing (PDF/DXF/STEP), material grade and thickness, seam lengths, quantity and, if any, the quality class (EN 1090 EXC, EN ISO 5817) and NDT requirements.
